\begin{abstract}
Generative policies pretrained on safe demonstrations often perform poorly in out-of-distribution environments because they inherit the training data's limited behavior support, and inference-time guidance only reweights samples from that fixed distribution rather than expanding what the policy can generate.We introduce \emph{Safe Flow Expansion} (SFE), an offline framework that iteratively self-generates actions from a conditional flow matching (CFM) policy, verifies them against finite-horizon discrete-time control barrier function (DCBF) conditions, and updates the policy to expand its set of valid actions. 
SFE builds on MPPI--DCBF, our proposed sampling-based planner that rejects rollouts leaving a convex nominal polytope before averaging. Under stated conditions, MPPI--DCBF certifies the executed step and provides demonstrations are modulated by safety parameter $\gamma$. We derive a conditional lower bound on the probability that a batch of generated actions contains a valid action. In 2D and 3D simulation and on Crazyflie hardware, the expanded policy achieves higher validity and mode coverage than pretrained and guided baselines. We also showed that it replans with far lower latency than online planners, and $\gamma$ tunes the conservativeness of both the planner and the policy.

\section{Introduction}
% \note{I think we need to stress that MPPI-DCBF can offer an on-line safety procedure in addition to off-line training data.}

Sampling-based predictive control (SBPC) is gaining traction as an optimal control framework. Completely general optimal control approaches often suffer from the computational complexity that is associated with solving stochastic Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman equations backward in time. SBPC is an alternative to gradient-based model predictive control (MPC), where a simple sampling procedure replaces a sophisticated nonlinear optimizer~\cite{posa2014direct}. 
Algorithms in this family include model predictive path integral control (MPPI)~\cite{williams2016aggressive}, predictive sampling~\cite{howell2022predictive}, Generative Predictive Control~\cite{Kurtz_GPC}, and cross-entropy methods~\cite{rubinstein1999cross}. MPPI, for example, circumvents computational complexity by employing forward sampling of stochastic dynamics combined with Monte Carlo approximation techniques~\cite{williams2016aggressive}. 

SBPC algorithms are particularly well-suited to recent advances in parallel computing architectures~\cite{mjx, makoviychuk2021isaac}, enabling them to scale to complex problems like in-hand dexterous manipulation~\cite{li2025drop}, legged locomotion~\cite{xue2025full}, and more~\cite{howell2022predictive, Kurtz_GPC}.

Despite these advantages, SBPCs such as MPPI, inherently struggle with safety guarantees. Safety constraints are typically incorporated indirectly through penalty terms in the path planning cost function~\cite{williams2017model, yin2023shield}. While this approach encourages safe behavior, it lacks explicit safety guarantees because the procedure relies on sampling and averaging. 

Even if individual sampled trajectories satisfy safety constraints, their weighted average may not. This limitation, motivates our goal of first integrating safety-critical constraints explicitly into SBPC frameworks. To this end, we introduce MPPI-DCBFs, which prevent such failure modes by restricting all nonzero-weight rollouts to a common convex nominal polytope before averaging.

A common method to ensure safety in collision-avoidance tasks is to impose finite-horizon CBF constraints directly within MPC~\cite{zeng2021enhancing}, under nonlinear dynamics and nonconvex obstacle geometry. However, the resulting optimization can be nonconvex and computationally heavy. This computational burden motivates a shift from online constrained optimization to learning-based methods, where complex planning is performed offline to curate datasets of safe and multi-modal behavior. Generative models, such as diffusion and flow matching (FM)~\cite{lipman2022flow}, can synthesize motion trajectories and action sequences from diverse, high-dimensional demonstrations~\cite{chi2023diffusion, Kurtz_GPC}. Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) has emerged as a promising alternative to diffusion-based modeling, offering competitive generation quality with efficient sampling~\cite{chisari2024learning, ye2024efficient}. 
% CFM is particularly useful in safety-critical motion planning and control, as it can generate high-fidelity, multi-modal trajectories with fewer inference time steps. 
% Maintaining this topological diversity is crucial to avoid committing to a single local solution that may become infeasible as the scene changes~\cite{de2024topology, shafiullah2022behavior}. 
However, they do not natively provide formal safety guarantees during rollout. 

Recent CFM-based works have further incorporated explicit safety constraints at inference time by trajectory regularization or constrained generation~\cite{li2026hardflow,utkarsh2026physics}, while MPC-based refinement has been used to improve local optimality of generated trajectories~\cite{mizuta2025unified}. However, these methods guide or refine samples from a fixed pretrained distribution. In OOD contexts, the pretrained model may assign low probability to safe and multi-modal behaviors, and aggressive guidance can further push generation away from its learned distribution~\cite{dandapanthula2026we}.

Consequently, a fundamental challenge in generative motion planning and control is to address this distribution shift by expanding the generable set of a pretrained policy toward safe and multi-modal regions. Recent work formalizes this as generable set expansion~\cite{de2026active} to expand the range of behaviors the FM model can generate. We extend this concept to a CFM model to discover new safe behaviors in robotics motion planning. To this end, we introduce the \textit{Safe Flow Expansion} framework, which expands the pretrained policy toward new safe behaviors using a safety-critical predictive planner and verifier mechanisms. Under the stated safety conditions, MPPI-DCBF can be deployed as an online receding-horizon controller while also generating certified demonstrations for offline CFM training.

\textbf{Contributions.} Our contributions are threefold:
(i) Safe Flow Expansion, an offline framework that expands a CFM policy's valid generable set by uncertainty-guided self-generation, finite-horizon DCBF verification, and continued pretraining, discovering valid OOD modes the pretrained policy misses.
(ii) MPPI-DCBF, an online planner that rejects sampled rollouts violating finite-horizon DCBF conditions before weighted averaging, preserving the safety certificate of the executed next state under control-affine dynamics and the entire predicted horizon for LTI systems.
(iii) An analysis carrying safety parameter $\gamma$ from planner to verifier to policy, giving, under stated assumptions, a per-context lower bound on the probability that a generated batch contains a valid action. We evaluate in 2D cluttered and pedestrian environments and on a 3D quadrotor, in simulation and on hardware.

\section{Preliminaries}
\subsection{Model Predictive Path Integral control}
In discrete time, at state $\xk\in\mathbb R^{n_x}$, MPPI optimizes a mean control sequence $\{ \uki \}_{i=0}^{H-1}$ in a convex admissible set $\mathcal U$ over horizon $H$ by forward sampling and Monte Carlo reweighting~\cite{williams2016aggressive}, warm started from the previous solution shifted one step, $\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{k+i\mid k}$. For rollout $m=1,\dots,M$ with perturbations $\varepsilon^{(m)}_{k+i}\!\sim\!\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)$, the sampled controls, states, and costs are
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
&\mathbf{u}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}=\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{k+i\mid k}+\varepsilon^{(m)}_{k+i},\label{eq:mppi_rollout}\\
&\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+i+1\mid k}=f\!\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k},\,\mathbf{u}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}\bigr),\\
&S^{(m)}\!=\!\phi\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+H\mid k}\bigr)
\!+\!\!\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}\Big(\!q\,\!\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}\bigr)\!+\!\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{u}^{(m)\top}_{k+i\mid k} R\,\mathbf{u}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}\Big)\label{eq:mppicost}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
with running cost $q: \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$, terminal cost $\phi : \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$,  control disturbance variance $\Sigma$, control weight $R \in \mathbb{R}^{n_u \times n_u}$. Soft-min weights with temperature $\lambda>0$, stabilized by subtracting $S_\text{min}$, define the update for $i\in\{1,\cdots,H-1\}$,
\begin{equation}
\omega_m\!:=\!\exp(-(S^{(m)}\!\!-\!S_{\min})/{\lambda}),\; S_{\min}\!:=\!\!\!\min_{\ell=1,\ldots,M}S^{(\ell)}.
\label{eq:mppi-weights}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Delta \mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}\!=\!
\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{M}\omega_m\,\varepsilon^{(m)}_{k+i}}{\sum_{m=1}^{M}\omega_m},
\mathbf{u}^*_{k+i\mid k}\!\leftarrow\!\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{k+i\mid k}\!+\!\Delta \mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}. \label{eq:mppi-update}
\end{equation}
of which only $\uk^\star$ is applied before replanning. This parallelizable sampling process makes MPPI well-suited to solving non-convex optimal control problems on modern GPU hardware. However, a key challenge arises in safety-critical applications. Prior work either biases the sampling distribution toward safety without certifying it~\cite{yin2022,yin2023shield}, or executes the first input of the lowest-cost rollout under a CBF filter~\cite{rabiee2024guaranteed}.

\subsection{Conditional Flow Matching for trajectory generation}
CFM is increasingly used in robotics for trajectory generation and policy generation \cite{Kurtz_GPC,mizuta2025unified}.
Trajectory generation learns a mapping from noise to entire collision-free trajectories via end-to-end supervised learning, but it can struggle to generalize to OOD environments and may require complete retraining \cite{ye2024efficient, dai2025safefm}. We train our CFM policy on MPPI-DCBF expert action sequences. Following the MPPI update in~\eqref{eq:mppi-update} let $\z:=\mathbf u^\star_{k:k+H-1|k}$. The model learns the distribution of $\z$ given the observation $\o$ and $\gamma$ (we denote this as the DCBF parameter or safety parameter).

For $\epsilon\!\sim\!\mathcal N(0,I_d)$ and $\z\!\sim\! p_{\mathrm{data}}$, we use an optimal transport conditional probability path $\x_t\!=\!(1-t)\epsilon+t\z$, whose sample-wise velocity is $\dot{\x}_t=\z-\epsilon$~\cite{lipman2022flow}. 
The conditional flow-matching objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta)=
\mathbb{E}_{\substack{
t\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1],\;
\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_d),\\
(\z,\o,\gamma)\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}
}}
\left[
\left\|
\mathbf v_t^\theta(\x_t\mid\o,\gamma)-(\z-\epsilon)
\right\|^2
\right].
\label{eq:cfm-loss}
\end{equation}
At inference, an action sequence (hereafter, an \emph{action}) is generated by integrating 
$\dot{\x}_t=\mathbf v_t^\theta(\x_t\mid\o,\gamma)$
from $\x_0\sim\mathcal N(0,I_d)$ to $t=1$, from which the first action is executed in receding horizon.

\subsection{Generable Set Expansion}
% In offline training, generative policies constrain actions to the distribution of their demonstrations. 
Recent work measures effective coverage by the \textit{$\tau$-generable set} $\Omega^\tau_\theta\!:=\!\{\x:p_1^\theta(\x)\ge\tau\}$, the region a FM model samples with non-negligible probability under a finite budget~\cite{de2026active}. Each expansion round self-generates candidates, labels them with a black-box verifier, and continues pretraining on the accepted samples. Candidates are acquired where the verifier is most uncertain, under a KL-regularized tilt of strength $\beta$ toward the current model. With calibrated verifier feedback~\cite{pasztor2024bandits}, finitely many verified queries then suffice, with high probability, for the generable set to contain samples that are reachable in boundedly many local steps~\cite{de2026active}. We adopt this view for a $\gamma$-conditioned CFM policy with a finite-horizon DCBF verifier in place of the black-box oracle, and Section~\ref{sec:analysis} provides the theoretical analysis. 


\subsection{Discrete-Time Control Barrier Functions}
Let $\mathcal{C}=\{\x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_x}:\; h(\x)\ge 0\}$ denote the safe set for a continuously differentiable $h$.
For the discrete‐time control‐affine system
\begin{equation}
    \x_{k+1}=f(\x_k)+g(\x_k)\u_k, \label{eq:affinesystem}
\end{equation}
A function $h$ is a discrete‐time control barrier function (DCBF) associated with the control system \eqref{eq:affinesystem} if there exists $\gamma\in(0,1]$ such that for all $\x_k$ there exists a control $\u_k$ with
\begin{equation}
    \Delta h(\x_k,\u_k)\;:=\;h(\x_{k+1})-h(\x_k)\;\ge\;-\gamma\,h(\x_k).
    \label{eq:dtcbf}
\end{equation}
If \eqref{eq:dtcbf} holds at every step, then \(h(\x_k)\!\ge\!(1-\gamma)^{k}\,h(\x_0)\), so the fixed set $\mathcal{C}$ is forward invariant~\cite{ames2014cbf,agrawal2017discrete}.

Let \(\piq:\mathbb R^{n_x} \to \mathbb R^d\) be the linear projection that maps states into the robot positions, \(\piq(\xki)=\qki, i=1,\cdots,H\). So the sets below are convex in the state, and linearize otherwise. For a convex configuration-space obstacle $j$, with closest boundary point $\p_j$ to the current position $\qk$and outward normal $\n_j$, the supporting hyperplane there gives the affine barrier function
\setlength{\belowdisplayskip}{3pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayshortskip}{3pt}
\begin{equation}
h_j^{\mathrm{aff}}(\x;\xk) :=
\frac{\n_j^\top(\q-\p_j)}{\n_j^\top(\q_{k\mid k}-\mathbf{p_j})}.
\label{eq:aff_barrier}
\end{equation}
Since $\n_j^\top(\qk-\p_j)>0$, the safe set for each horizon $i$ can be defined as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{aff}}_{i,j}=\{\x \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \mid h_j^{\mathrm{aff}}(\x;\xk)\ge (1-\gamma)^i\},\label{eq:aff_safeset}
\end{equation}
is a convex half-space. Iterating the condition \eqref{eq:dtcbf} from $i=0$ with the normalization $h_j^{\mathrm{aff}}(\xk;\xk)=1$ yields
\begin{equation}
h_j^{\mathrm{aff}}(\xki;\xk) \ge (1-\gamma)^i. \quad i = 1, \ldots, H.
\label{eq:safetyspecification}
\end{equation}
It applies to any convex obstacle through its closest boundary point and outward normal, and to non-convex ones through a convex hull or decomposition.

\noindent \textbf{Nominal Polytope for MPPI safety.} A \textit{nominal polytope} generalize this to multiple obstacles. Let $\mathcal O(\xk)$ index the obstacles observed at $\xk$. We define the nominal ego-centric (\textit{i.e.}, defined as a function of $\xk$) safety function
\begin{equation}
    h^{\mathrm{poly}}(\x;\xk) = \min_{j\in\mathcal O(\xk)} h^{\mathrm{aff}}_j(\x;\x_{k\mid k})
\end{equation}
Accordingly, the safe set for each horizon,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{poly}}_i=\{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \mid h^{\mathrm{poly}}(\x;\xk)\ge (1-\gamma)^i\},\label{eq:poly_safeset}
\end{equation}
The free space around multiple obstacles is generally nonconvex, but $\mathcal C_i^{\mathrm{poly}}$ is a convex inner approximation set obtained by intersecting half-spaces separating robot from obstacles. These sets are nested, \(\mathcal C_1^{\mathrm{poly}}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq \mathcal C_H^{\mathrm{poly}}\), and reconstructed at each replanning step.
Since $h_j^{\mathrm{aff}}\geq0$ places $\q$ beyond the supporting hyperplane at $\p_j$, every state in $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{poly}}_i$ has its position outside the interior of every sensed obstacle. A certified trajectory is therefore collision-free in the sensed scene. We claim neither forward invariance of these time-varying sets nor recursive feasibility, relying on the condition below. Assumption~\ref{assumption_1} states it, keeping the set of certified trajectories non-empty.
% Furthermore, it is constructed as a bounded set by incorporating bounding faces within a sensing range, which will be discussed further.
% While the proper design of terminal ingredients (\textit{i.e.,} a terminal set and cost) can formally guarantee recursive feasibility in traditional MPC, the non-convex nature of the resulting optimization problem often makes real-time implementation computationally intractable \cite{rawlings2020model}. SBPC methods such as MPPI face similar challenges. Rather than proving recursive feasibility, our approach ensures safety by enforcing DCBF constraints at each time step, which relies on the weaker but more practical condition of pointwise feasibility, formalized in the following assumption.

\begin{assumption}[Pointwise feasibility]
\label{assumption_1}
For the discrete-time control affine system \eqref{eq:affinesystem}, for every replanning state $\xk$, and safety parameter $\gamma \in (0,1]$, 
there exists a control sequence\footnote{Assumption~\ref{assumption_1} is the condition for pointwise feasibility of the MPPI-DCBF algorithm at the current state; it ensures that the set of DCBF-certified trajectories for the planner to find is non-empty.}
$\{ \uki \}_{i=0}^{H-1}\in\mathcal U^H$ such that the resulting trajectory $\{\xki\}_{i=1}^{H}$ lies within the sequence of safe sets defined in~\eqref{eq:poly_safeset}, i.e., $\xki \in \mathcal{C}_i^{\mathrm{poly}}$:
\begin{equation}
h^{\mathrm{poly}}(\xki;\xk)\ge(1-\gamma)^i,
\quad i=1,\ldots,H.\label{eq:nominalpolytope}
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\section{Problem Definition}
\label{sec:problemdefinition}
To generate safety-critical policies, we pose safe planning as a constrained optimization problem. Let $h(\cdot;\xk)$ be any ego-centric safety function, whose nonnegative superlevel set contains only states in free space, fixed over the horizon and normalized by $h(\xk;\xk)=1$. The formulation is \textit{agnostic} to that choice:

\setlength{\abovedisplayskip}{2pt}
\setlength{\abovedisplayshortskip}{2pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayskip}{2pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayshortskip}{2pt}
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:problemdef}
\begin{alignat}{2}
& &&\min_{\mathbf{u}_{k:k+H-1\mid k}}\quad
 \mathbb{E}\!\left[
J(\mathbf{x}_{k:k+H\mid k},
  \mathbf{u}_{k:k+H-1\mid k})
\right]\\
& \text{s.t.}\;
&& \mathbf{x}_{k+i+1\mid k}\!=\!f(\mathbf{x}_{k+i\mid k},\mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}),\; i\!=\!0,\ldots,H-1,\\
&&& \mathbf{x}_{k\mid k}\!=\!\mathbf{x}_k,
\mathbf{x}_{k:k+H\mid k}\!\in\!\mathcal{X}^{H+1},
\mathbf{u}_{k:k+H-1\mid k}\!\in\!\mathcal{U}^{H},\\
&&& h(\mathbf{x}_{k+i\mid k};\mathbf{x}_k) \!\ge\! (1-\gamma)^i,\; i\!=\!1,\ldots,H.
\label{eq:poly}
\end{alignat}
\end{subequations}

Direct optimization using a non-convex $h$ does not generally guarantee recursive feasibility for this problem and often makes real-time implementation computationally intractable~\cite{rawlings2020model}. On the other hand, from the view of set invariance, $\gamma$ modulates the trade-off between \textit{safety and feasibility}~\cite{zeng2021enhancing}. For example, a small $\gamma$ restricts the safety function to higher values, at the cost of reduced feasibility.

We propose MPPI with DCBF-based sample rejection (MPPI-DCBF), which enforces the safety specification~\eqref{eq:poly} with $h=h^\text{poly}$ inside the MPPI sampling step, which is designed to guarantee the forward invariance of the sequence of time-varying safe sets $\mathcal{C}_i^{\mathrm{poly}}$ defined in \eqref{eq:poly_safeset}. This is achieved by integrating the DCBF condition directly into the MPPI sampling step. We reject any sampled rollout that violates the condition; consequently, we compute the optimal control sequence \eqref{eq:mppi-update} as a convex combination of only rollouts certified to be safe. Under the pointwise feasibility assumption (Assumption.~\ref{assumption_1}), we can formally analyze the properties of the MPPI-DCBF averaging process.

\begin{proposition} \label{prop:1}
    For the discrete-time control affine system \eqref{eq:affinesystem}, if all contributing rollouts $(m)$ satisfy $\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+1\mid k} \in \mathcal{C}_1^{\mathrm{poly}}$, then the averaged state $\mathbf{x}^*_{k+1|k}$ under the MPPI-DCBF control is also guaranteed to be in the safe set $\mathcal{C}_1^{\mathrm{poly}}$.
    
    % \begin{proof}
    % Under the control affine dynamics, the state $\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+1\mid k}$ in the next time step is an affine function of the control inputs. Since the admissible inputs are a convex polytope and the control update of MPPI is a convex combination of control perturbations~\eqref{eq:mppi-update}, the resulting state $\mathbf{x}^*_{k+1\mid k}$ is a convex combination of the rollout states:
    % \begin{align}

Moreover, since the safe set $\mathcal{C}_1^{\mathrm{poly}}$ is a convex set, any convex combination of points within it must also lie within the set. Thus, if $\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+1\mid k} \in \mathcal{C}_1^{\mathrm{poly}}$ for all $m$, it follows that $\mathbf{x}^*_{k+1|k} \in \mathcal{C}_1^{\mathrm{poly}}$.

\subsection{Multi-step Safety Verification}
We define a deterministic verifier that tests whether a queried action admits a finite-horizon DCBF certificate (\textit{i.e.,} multi-step safety).

\noindent \textbf{Verifier Polytope for Expansion.}
The nominal polytope~\eqref{eq:poly_safeset} is shared by all nonzero-weight rollouts, which makes the averaging argument work. In a nonconvex free space, however, a single shared region may exclude collision-free rollouts in other local modes, even when enlarged~\cite{deits2015computing}. Therefore, to expand a generative policy into these unexplored safe modes, we design the verifier keeps the same conditions but builds the separating faces around the queried action, therefore verify the actions the original planner excludes (See Fig.~\ref{fig:pedestrian}(a)).

For a queried action \(\hat{\mathbf u}\), let
\(\{\xki\}_{i=0}^{H}\) denotes the resulting trajectory. For each convex configuration-space obstacle \(j\in\mathcal O(\xk)\), represented as \(\mathcal O_j:=\{\q\in\mathbb R^d:\|\q-\oj\|_2\le r_j\},\)
the verifier constructs a separating hyperplane \(\mathbf a_j^\top(\mathbf q-\qk)\le m_j,\)
by solving the maximization problem
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\max_{\aj, m_j}\ & m_j\\
\text{s.t. }\ & \aj^\top(\qki-\qk)\le\big(1-(1-\gamma)^i\big)m_j \; (i=1,\dots,H),\\
& r_j\|\aj\|_2\le \aj^\top(\oj-\qk)-m_j,\\
&\|\aj\|_2\le1,\ 0< m_{\min} \le m_j.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:socp}
\end{equation}
Problem~\eqref{eq:socp} is a second-order cone program (SOCP), solved with Clarabel through CVXPY~\cite{goulart2026clarabel}. Specifically, the first trajectory constraint imposes the safety specification in \eqref{eq:poly} with \(h(\xki;\xk)=1-\aj^\top(\qki-\qk)/m_j\) equal to 1 at the current position and is required to remain at least $(1-\gamma)^i$. The obstacle-separation constraint follows from
\[
\inf_{\q\in\mathcal O_j}\aj^\top(\q-\qk)
=
\aj^\top(\oj-\qk)-r_j\|\aj\|_2
\ge m_j.
\]
The remaining constraints fix the scale of the normal and impose the strictly positive margin $m_{\min}\!>\!0$. With $(\aj^\star,m_j^\star)$ a solution for obstacle $j$. The resulting verifier polytope \footnote{For brevity, we abbreviate the \(H\) steps of predicted control sequence (or action) as $\{ \hat{\u}_{k+i\mid k}\}_{i=0}^{H-1}$ as \(\hat{\mathbf u}\). Also, we add auxiliary bounding faces around the sensing region so that $\mathcal P$ bounded (12  faces in Fig.~\ref{fig:pedestrian}(a)).} for a queried action is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P(\hat{\mathbf u};\o,\gamma):=\nspace{-10}\bigcap_{j\in\mathcal O(\xk)}\nspace{-10}\{
\q\in\mathbb R^d:
{\aj^\star}^{\!\top}(\q-\qk)\le m_j^\star\}.
\label{eq:verifierpolytope}
\end{equation}

    \vspace{-6.0mm}

Colors show the four modes: above, below, left, and right. Smaller $\beta$ puts more weight on uncertain actions and discovers new trajectories.}

\end{figure}

Having formulated the optimization problem to verify the existence of a multi-step safety specification, we formally define the \textit{validity} of a queried action.
\begin{definition}
\label{def:verifier}
Let \(v_\gamma(\hat{\mathbf u};\o) \in \{0, 1\}\) be a indicator function that equals $1$ if and only if~\eqref{eq:socp} is feasible for \emph{every} $j\in\mathcal O(\xk)$ at action $\hat{\mathbf u}$, context $\o$, and safety parameter $\gamma$.
The \textit{valid action set} is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)
    :=
    \left\{
    \hat{\mathbf u}\in\mathcal U^H:
    v_\gamma(\hat{\mathbf u};\o)=1
    \right\},
    \label{eq:verifier-certified-set}
\end{equation}
and action \(\hat{\mathbf u} \in \Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)\) is a \textit{valid} action that guarantees the existence of a verifier polytope~\eqref{eq:verifierpolytope}.
\end{definition}

Thus, a predicted trajectory of a valid action is collision-free, and the feasibility of~\eqref{eq:socp} is decided at each planning step. Under the condition below, every action satisfying the nominal polytope constraints~\ref{eq:nominalpolytope} is a valid action.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:nominal-verifier}
Fix $(\o,\gamma)$ and suppose the configuration-space
obstacles are represented as above. Let $\p_j$ and the
outward unit normal $\n_j$ define the nominal face, and let \(d_j:=\n_j^\top(\qk-\p_j)=\|\qk-\oj\|_2-r_j>0.\)
If $m_{\min}\le\min_{j\in\mathcal O(\xk)}d_j$, then, for every
$\hat{\mathbf u}\in\mathcal U^H$ whose resulting trajectory satisfies
\[
h^{\mathrm{poly}}(\xki;\xk)\ge(1-\gamma)^i,
\quad i=1,\ldots,H,
\]
the choice $(\aj,m_j)=(-\n_j,d_j)$ is feasible
for~\eqref{eq:socp}, and hence
$\hat{\mathbf u}\in\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$.
\end{proposition}
This implication holds for MPPI-DCBF under Assumption~\ref{assumption_1} and the LTI conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:2}. For a general control-affine system, each queried action must be verified separately. 
In the following proposition, we show the effect of $\gamma$ during the verification process.
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:gamma-effect}
For any $\o$ and
$0<\gamma_1\le\gamma_2\le1$,
\setlength{\abovedisplayskip}{2pt}
\setlength{\abovedisplayshortskip}{2pt}
\begin{equation}
\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma_1)
\subseteq
\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma_2).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
For every horizon $i$,
$1-(1-\gamma)^i$ is nondecreasing in $\gamma$.
Thus, any valid action at $\gamma_1$ also satisfies the trajectory constraints at $\gamma_2$, no other constraint of~\eqref{eq:socp} involves $\gamma$. \hfill$\blacksquare$

While Proposition~\ref{prop:nominal-verifier} formally guarantees that the MPPI-DCBF planner gives valid action priors, it inherently yields a subset of the distribution of all possible safe actions at a given context and required safety level (\textit{i.e.,} $\gamma$). This fundamental limitation strongly motivates us to expand the generative policy distribution beyond the initial demonstrations to discover a broader range of valid actions.

\subsection{Safe Flow Expansion}
We introduce Safe Flow Expansion (Alg.~\ref{alg:sfe}) for the CFM model, which acquires missing safe behavioral modes by first pretraining on the safe action support provided by safety-critical SBPC (\textit{i.e.,} MPPI-DCBF) and iteratively increasing the probability mass on uncertain multi-step safe actions. 

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Safe Flow Expansion}
\label{alg:sfe}
\small
\LinesNumbered
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{pretrained CFM $\theta_0$; verifier $v_{\gamma_k}$; dynamics $f$;
execution cost $C$; rounds $N$; success target $P$;
proposal and verification budgets $K\ge B$}
\KwOut{expanded CFM $\theta_N$}
$\mathcal B_0\gets\varnothing$\;
\For{$n\gets0$ \KwTo $N-1$}{
$\sigma_n\gets\textsc{UpdateUncertainty}(\mathcal B_n)$;\quad
$\mathcal D_n,\mathcal S_n\gets\varnothing$\;
\Repeat{$|\mathcal S_n|\ge P$}{
roll out $\theta_n$ in parallel\;
\ForEach{replanning step $k$ with context $(\o_k,\gamma_k)$}{
draw $\hat{\mathbf u}^{(1:B)}$ from $K$ proposals
by~\eqref{eq:importancesampling}\;
$y_j\gets v_{\gamma_k}(\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j)};\o_k)$,
$\;\mathcal J_k^+\gets\{j:y_j=1\}$\;
$\mathcal D_n\gets\mathcal D_n\cup
\{(\o_k,\gamma_k,\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j)},y_j)\}_{j=1}^{B}$\;
\lIf{$\mathcal J_k^+=\varnothing$}{\textsc{RestartRollout}()}
$j^\star\gets\arg\min_{j\in\mathcal J_k^+}
C(\o_k,\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j)})$\;
$\mathbf u_k\gets[\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j^\star)}]_0$,
$\;\mathbf x_{k+1}\gets f(\mathbf x_k,\mathbf u_k)$\;
}
add every restart-free rollout to $\mathcal S_n$\;
}
$\mathcal B_{n+1}\gets\textsc{UpdateBuffer}(\mathcal B_n,\mathcal D_n)$;\quad

$\theta_{n+1}\gets\textsc{UpdateCFM}(\theta_n,\mathcal D_n^{+})$\;
}
\Return{$\theta_N$}\;
% \vskip -0.1 true in
\end{algorithm}

Concretely, for a fixed \((\o,\gamma)\), the policy at expansion round \(n\) induces the \textit{conditional generable set} associated with the probability density lower bound \(\tau\) defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_{\theta_n}^{\tau}(\o,\gamma)
    :=\{
    \hat{\mathbf u}\in\mathcal U^H:
    p_1^{\theta_n}(\hat{\mathbf u}\mid\o,\gamma)\ge\tau
    \},
    \label{eq:conditional-generable-set}
\end{equation}
where $p_1^{\theta_n}(\mathbf u\mid\o,\gamma)$ denotes the conditional policy density at round $n$. The conditional generable set contains actions to which the policy assigns density at least $\tau$. Here, we recover the pre-trained conditional generable set by setting \(n=0\).

To self-generate samples informative about multi-step safety while staying near the current policy \(\theta_n\), at expansion round \(n\),
we sample\footnote{The exact Gibbs solution of
\(
p_n \in \arg\max_q \{ \mathbb{E}_q[\sigma_n(\phi_s(\hat\u))] - \beta \text{KL}(q \parallel p_1^{\theta_n})\},
\) with $\text{KL}$ denotes Kullback--Leibler divergence.}
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf u}
\sim
p_1^{\theta_n}(\hat{\mathbf u}\mid\o,\gamma)
\exp\!\left(
\sigma_n\!\left(\phi_s(\hat{\mathbf u};\o,\gamma)\right)/\beta
\right)
/Z_n(\o,\gamma)
,
\label{eq:importancesampling}
\end{equation}
where $Z_n(\o,\gamma)$ is the normalizing constant \footnote{Define \(
Z_n(\o,\gamma)\!=\!\int_{\mathcal U^H}
p_1^{\theta_n}(\mathbf u\!\mid\!\o,\!\gamma)\exp\!\left(
\sigma_n\!\left(\phi_s(\mathbf u;\o,\!\gamma)\right)/\beta
\right)
d\mathbf u.
\)}, $\phi_s$ denotes the penultimate layer feature of the conditional flow vector field evaluated at the fixed flow-noise level $s$, and $\sigma_n$ is the kernel posterior uncertainty returned by \textsc{UpdateUncertainty} in Alg.~\ref{alg:sfe}. We modeled $\sigma_n$ as the posterior standard deviation of a Gaussian process fitted on the features $\phi_s$ to the binary observations in the buffer. For $\phi=\phi_s(\hat{\mathbf u};\o,\gamma)$, define $[\mathbf K_n]_{ij}=k(\phi_i,\phi_j)$ and $[\mathbf k_n(\phi)]_i=k(\phi_i,\phi)$. The posterior variance is
\begin{equation}
\sigma_n^2(\phi)=k(\phi,\phi)-\mathbf k_n(\phi)^{\!\top}\left(\mathbf K_n+\nu I\right)^{-1}\mathbf k_n(\phi),
\label{eq:gp-uncertainty}
\end{equation}
where $\nu\!>\!0$ is regularization parameter. We use its posterior standard deviation as a feature-space uncertainty score.

%At the beginning of each expansion round, \textsc{UpdateUncertainty} fits the verifier uncertainty $\sigma_n$ from the current buffer $\mathcal B_n$ 
At each replanning step $k$, SFE self-generates a batch of actions from the current CFM policy using the GP posterior uncertainty $\sigma_n$ in ~\eqref{eq:importancesampling} within the parallel rollouts. The verifier $v_{\gamma_k}$, defined by the finite-horizon DCBF conditions in Def.~\ref{def:verifier}, separates these queried actions $\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j)}$ into valid (\textit{i.e.,} $j\in\mathcal J_k^+$) and invalid sets, and adds both sets to $\mathcal D_n$. If no valid action exists, the rollout restarts.

The execution cost \(C\) ranks valid action candidates, from which the first action with minimum cost is executed~\footnote{One can set this cost as expert actions \cite{ross2011reduction} or restrict execution to a safe admissible action set \cite{alshiekh2018safe}.}. Even when $C$ is shared across, queried actions depend on $\gamma$ through the admissible set $\mathcal J_k^+$, thereby preserving the semantics of $\gamma$ (\textit{i.e.,} safety--performance tradeoff) through policy expansion. 

We repeat this data acquisition until we gather $P$ successful trajectories. Both valid and invalid sets are then added to the buffer through \textsc{UpdateBuffer}, from which \textsc{UpdateUncertainty} constructs $\sigma_{n+1}$ in the next round. Finally, \textsc{UpdateCFM} uses hierarchical sampling~\cite{idrissi2022simple} among valid queried actions from $\mathcal D_n^+$ uniformly over $\gamma$ and successful trajectories, and regresses the CFM loss~\eqref{eq:cfm-loss} on these uncertainty-guided data.
 
Because the buffer \(\mathcal B_n\) pools observations from finite contexts at each round, \(\sigma_n\) may prioritize an action due to novelty in unrelated contexts. However, no finite buffer can cover the space of planning contexts--the same issues that have arisen that make conditional generative planners struggle to generalize to OOD environments without retraining \cite{ye2024efficient, dai2025safefm}. We therefore use the buffer only as an acquisition heuristic to actively explore unvisited modes \cite{de2026active}, while it can affect the query but not validity.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol and Implementation}
\noindent\textbf{Evaluation protocol.}
% \noindent \textbf{Train and test data details}
% The specific training and test configurations are as follows:
MPPI-DCBF generates all pretraining data under 2D and 3D double-integrator dynamics and a sampling time of $\Delta t = 0.1s$. Across four tasks, we structure the evaluation into three phases: initial in-distribution training, SFE (Alg.~\ref{alg:sfe}) in OOD environments, and final OOD testing, with the start, goal, and dynamics fixed. 

In \emph{configuration-specific adaptation}, expansion and evaluation use the same OOD obstacle configuration (2D cluttered environment and 3D single-sphere environment). In \emph{cross-configuration OOD generalization}, evaluation uses independently sampled OOD configurations that are not used during expansion (2D dynamic-pedestrian environment and the 3D cluttered environment). To account for the varying difficulty of the four tasks, we use a task-specific number $P$ of successful trajectories, fixed across rounds. After each expansion round $n$, we independently evaluate $\theta_n$ with a 16-step ODE solver on 100 test rollouts for each task and value of $\gamma$.

Data generation and training used an NVIDIA H100 NVL GPU, all online tests used an NVIDIA RTX 4060 Ti GPU.


\noindent \textbf{Task configurations:}
In the \emph{2D cluttered environment}, The robot starts at \((0.3,0.3)\) and navigates toward the fixed goal at \((4.7,4.7)\) in a \(5\,\mathrm{m}\times5\,\mathrm{m}\) taskspace. The CFM model is pretrained on an MPPI-DCBF policy from a random initial position to a fixed goal position, total 200k actions of length $H$ as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:2d_cluttered}, in a nominal environment with regularly sized circular obstacles. We tested this by replacing the central obstacles with a previously unseen large obstacle to see whether SFE can learn long-horizon, collision-free behavior and avoid getting trapped in local minima.
        
In the \emph{2D dynamic-pedestrian environment}, the robot starts at \((0,0)\) and moves to \((6,6)\), with the simulated pedestrian environment modeled by the social force model~\cite{helbing1995social}, where each pedestrian is a modeled reactive dynamical system whose acceleration exponentially grows as agents approach. The CFM model is pretrained on an MPPI-DCBF policy in distribution with 20 pedestrians moving at \(0.5\!-\!1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}\), whereas evaluation uses 40 pedestrians moving at \(1.0\!-\!2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}\). In these challenging dynamic tasks, the policy conditions on the 10-step history of local observations $h^\text{poly}$, unlike other tasks. We tested this to capture how SFE can learn highly reactive behaviors under dynamic environments.
% 10 time steps, 32 angular bins, and 100 radial bins.

For the \emph{3D hardware environments}, we deploy online 3D motion planning on a \textit{Crazyflie} quadrotor. The robot starts at \((-2.5,1.5,0.9)\) to \((0.7,-1.5,0.9)\) in a \(4\,\mathrm{m}\times4\,\mathrm{m}\times1.5\,\mathrm{m}\) taskspace. A Vicon motion-capture system provides quadrotor and obstacle poses at $50$ Hz. The policy replans at $10$ Hz, streaming the acceleration command at $100$ Hz as the feedforward component of a full-state setpoint.

For both single-sphere and cluttered environments, the CFM model is pretrained in environments with $4-8$ randomly positioned cylinder obstacles. In the former configuration, a sphere is placed at the midpoint between the start and the goal; in the latter, it uses $6$ independent randomized spheres. For the mode coverage test (Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}), we fixed the test configuration with $6$ spheres to compare the pretrained model, the expanded model with $5$ rounds, full rounds of $10$, and the baseline method. 

\noindent \textbf{Implementation details:} 
The base CFM model uses a flow-matching velocity network of a single MLP with 3 hidden layers, conditioned on the safety parameter and the context: the relative goal position, the velocity, and local observation at the current time step $h^\text{poly}$ (or history in pedestrian tasks) within a \(2\mathrm{m}\) sensing range encoded as a discretized grid. Specifically, we used a convolutional neural network (CNN) to preserve the radial resolution while downsampling the angular axes, then compress the result into a 64-dim observation token. \subsection{Safe Flow Expansion Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

This section connects the MPPI-DCBF demonstrations to the initial set for SFE and a conditional lower bound on valid-action availability (Details on the fixed-context analysis model and generable set coverage are deferred to~\Cref{app:sfe-analysis}). All results apply separately to each fixed $(\o,\gamma)$. Extending the analysis across the full observation and safety parameter space remains future work.

For a validity threshold $p_{\min}\in(0,1)$ and $\epsilon\in(0,1-p_{\min})$, we denote a measurable $S_0\subseteq\mathcal U^H$ an \textit{initial safe set} if $\operatorname{Vol}(S_0)$ is well defined and positive, $S_0\subseteq\Omega^{\tau}_{\theta_0}(\o,\gamma)\cap\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$, and $\inf_{\hat{\mathbf u}\in S_0}\varsigma(g(\phi_s(\hat{\mathbf u};\o,\gamma))) \ge p_{\min}+\epsilon$. Let $R_\epsilon$ denote the induced action-space one-step reachability operator of~\cite[Eq.~(19)]{de2026active}, with $\mathcal X=\mathcal U^H$ and $\phi=\phi_s(\cdot;\o,\gamma)$. We use  $R_\epsilon^r$ for $r$ repeated applications of this operator.

The MPPI-DCBF demonstrations provide only finitely many verified actions, which by themselves have zero volume in the action space and therefore cannot form the initial set required by the expansion analysis. The following lemma bridges this gap, where strict feasibility and continuity enlarge each verified action into a small safe neighborhood, whose union provides a nonzero-volume initial safe set for SFE.

\begin{lemma}[Initial safe set from MPPI-DCBF]
\label{lem:seed}
Under the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:nominal-verifier}, let $Q\ge1$ and let $\{\hat{\mathbf u}_0^{(q)}\}_{q=1}^{Q}\subset\operatorname{int}(\mathcal U^H)$
be a finite set of MPPI-DCBF actions satisfying~\eqref{eq:nominalpolytope}
strictly, with $p_1^{\theta_0}(\hat{\mathbf u}_0^{(q)}\mid\o,\gamma)>\tau$ and
$\varsigma(g(\phi_s(\hat{\mathbf u}_0^{(q)};\o,\gamma)))>p_{\min}+\epsilon$.
If the dynamics used by the planner in~\eqref{eq:affinesystem} are continuous in the state and action,
% If the resulting finite-horizon trajectory depends continuously on $\hat{\mathbf u}$ at these actions, 
and
$p_1^{\theta_0}(\cdot\mid\o,\gamma)$ and $\varsigma\circ g\circ\phi_s$ are
continuous, then there exist radii $\rho_q>0$ such that
$S_0:=\bigcup_q B_{\rho_q}(\hat{\mathbf u}_0^{(q)})$ is an initial seed set \footnote{Here, \(B_\rho(\bar{\mathbf u})\) denotes the open Euclidean ball \(\left\{\hat{\mathbf u}\!\in\!\mathcal U^H\!:\!\|\hat{\mathbf u}\!-\!\bar{\mathbf u}\|_2\!<\!\rho\right\}\).}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By continuity of the dynamics in~\eqref{eq:affinesystem}, the induced
finite-horizon trajectory map is continuous in $\hat{\mathbf u}$. 
Since all relevant conditions are strict, they remain true for actions sufficiently close to each $\hat{\mathbf u}_0^{(q)}$. Choose each $\rho_q$ small enough that its ball remains inside $\mathcal U^H$ and preserves these conditions. \Cref{prop:nominal-verifier} then places every such ball in $\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:seed} does not require the MPPI-DCBF demonstrations to cover all $\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$. It only requires a finite set of actions that satisfies the strict conditions and remains valid under sufficiently small action changes.

Starting from this initial safe set, Lemma~\ref{lem:coverage} guarantees that after $T^\star=rT$ sequential query and model-update steps in Appendix~\ref{app:sfe-analysis} at a fixed $(\o,\gamma)$, the coverage event $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{cov}}\!:=\!\{R_\epsilon^r(S_0)\!\subseteq\!\Omega_{\theta_{T^\star}}^\tau\!(\o,\gamma)\}$ holds with probability at least $1-\delta$, \textit{i.e.,} $\Pr(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{cov}})\ge1-\delta$. This gives the following sampling guarantee.

\begin{corollary}[Probability of valid action after expansion]
\label{thm:safe-action-availability}
Under the setting above, let $r\!\ge\!1$ be an integer and define
$\mathcal A_{\gamma,r}(\o)\!:=\!R_\epsilon^r(S_0)\cap\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$
and $m_{\gamma,r}(\o):=\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal A_{\gamma,r}(\o))$. Then $m_{\gamma,r}(\o)\ge\operatorname{Vol}(S_0)>0$, and $m_{\gamma,r}(\o)$ is nondecreasing in $r$. 
Conditioned on the expansion history, independently sample $J\ge1$ actions from $p_1^{\theta_{T^\star}}(\cdot\mid\o,\gamma)$. Accounting for both the expansion process and the $J$ samples, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\Pr\!\left[\exists\,\!j\!\le\! J\!:\! v_\gamma(\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j)};\!\o)\!=\!1\right]
% &\!\ge\!
% (1-\delta)\!\left[1-\!\bigl(1\!-\!\tau m_{\gamma,r}(\o)\!\bigr)^{J}\right]\!\\
\!\ge\!
(1-\delta)\!\left[1\!-\!e^{\!-J\tau m_{\gamma,r}(\o)}\right]\!\!.
\label{eq:main1}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By definition, the reachability operator contains its input and preserves set inclusion. Therefore, it holds that $S_0\subseteq\!R_\epsilon^r(S_0)\subseteq\! R_\epsilon^{r+1}(S_0)$. Intersecting with $\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)\supseteq S_0$ gives the volume bound. On the coverage event $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{cov}}$, every action in $\mathcal A_{\gamma,r}(\o)$ is a valid action and has density at least $\tau$. Independence of the $J$ samples gives the conditional bound \(1-\left(1-\tau m_{\gamma,r}(\o)\right)^J\), using \(1\!-\!x\!\le \!e^{-x}\), together with $\Pr(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{cov}})\ge1-\delta$ gives~\eqref{eq:main1}.
\end{proof}

The lower bound in~\eqref{eq:main1} is nondecreasing with $r$ as $m_{\gamma,r}(\o)$ is nondecreasing by the monotonicity of the reachability operator as described in Lemma~\ref{lem:coverage}, for fixed $(\o,\gamma,S_0,\epsilon,\tau,\delta,J)$~\footnote{This conclusion neither assumes that the conditional generable set for one $r$ is contained in the next nor uses the same coverage condition for different $r$. The claim applies only to the guaranteed lower bound, and it does not claim that the validity of each later CFM policy must increase.}. Also, not only the effect of $r$ but also the effect of $\gamma$ can be noted. Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-effect} establishes an inclusion between valid action sets $\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$ at different values of $\gamma$, whereas Corollary~\ref{thm:safe-action-availability} applies separately to each fixed $(\o,\gamma)$~\footnote{Changing $\gamma$ may also change the conditional policy, learned representation, initial safe set, and reachability operator.}. Therefore, Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-effect} by itself does not imply an inclusion between the corresponding reachable or conditional generable sets, and hence does not imply monotonicity of $m_{\gamma,r}(\o)$ or~\eqref{eq:main1} in $\gamma$.

\section{Conclusion}
Safe Flow Expansion raises verifier validity over the pretrained policy in four OOD environments (Fig.~\ref{fig:validity}). On hardware experiments, it discovers new modes absent from the demonstrations (Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}). With self-generation and verification offline, the expanded policy replans at a fraction of the baseline methods. We also demonstrated that the safety parameter $\gamma$ tunes both the online planner and the expanded policy along a clearance/time-to-goal frontier (Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff}).

Learning an optimal $\gamma$ schedule directly from data remains an interesting open problem (\textit{e.g.,} rather than a fixed $\gamma$ during deployment). Such dynamic scheduling would allow the system to tightly trade off safety and performance, which can be critical in real-world tasks where the robot must adjust caution to context.

Under the assumptions of~\Cref{sec:analysis}, Corollary~\ref{thm:safe-action-availability} provides lower bounds, with high probability, at a fixed context and $\gamma$, on the probability that a generated batch contains a valid action. Future work could explore combining our expanded policy with a lightweight run-time safety filter to provide hard guarantees. Our next step is to scale our framework to systems where expert demonstrations are prohibitively expensive, such as safety-critical manipulation, continuing the manipulator motion planning in~\ref{fig:robotarm}.

% Our next step is to scale the framework to systems with extremely large state spaces or where expert demonstrations are prohibitively expensive. In parallel, while our method robustly expands the safe action distribution, tuning the online execution cost $C$ can still shift the distribution away from the prior actions, which remains an important challenge.
