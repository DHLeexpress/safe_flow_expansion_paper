\begin{abstract}
A tight connection between generative control policies and sampling-based predictive control (SBPC) has recently gained attention in robotics. Enabled by modern computing architectures, sampling-based predictive control can generate multi-modal action sequences under model-based safety constraints, whereas conditional flow matching amortizes these behaviors into a fast generative policy. However, a pretrained generative policy can assign little probability to safe action windows in out-of-distribution (OOD). To address these issues, we propose the offline Safe Flow Expansion framework built upon model predictive path integral control (MPPI) and conditional flow matching (CFM). With the safety-conditioned receding-horizon control distribution learned from MPPI demonstrations, our framework self-generates and uses deterministic verification to collect finite-horizon DCBF certificate actions. We evaluate the performance of our adaptive design in safety-critical settings in both 2D and 3D unseen environments.
% Sampling-based predictive control (SBPC) algorithms, such as model predictive path integral (MPPI), have become a popular method for robotic motion planning applications due to their computational efficiency on modern computing architectures. However, it is difficult to enforce hard safety constraints for such techniques because they average over samples to realize the final set of control inputs. While individual samples may satisfy a given constraint, the sample average can violate the safety requirement. Similarly, diffusion and flow matching algorithms have become a popular way to generate robot motion trajectories. In this paper, we introduce a novel strategy to learn a conditional generative model that allows for online tuning of a safety parameter. As for sampling-based controllers, it is difficult to enforce hard safety-critical constraints in these methods. To address these limitations, this paper proposes a safety-critical framework for SBPCs and generative trajectory generation, based on the framework of discrete-time control barrier functions (DCBFs). Our DCBF-based method ensures safety during both the sampling and averaging phases of SBPCs, efficiently producing a rich dataset of multi-modal, collision-free behaviors. We then train a conditional flow matching model on these data to learn a generative policy that can be modulated at inference time by a single safety parameter. We demonstrate our approach for cluttered environments for a double integrator and a quadrotor simulation. Our results indicate that the proposed MPPI-DCBF framework consistently achieves superior safety guarantees compared to baseline MPPI methods, together with a context-aware conditional flow matching model, paving the way for real-time, safe SBPC and generative robotic control.
% We study whether a flow-matching control policy, pretrained from a limited set of safe expert demonstrations, can be actively expanded into new safe behavioral modes without sacrificing closed-loop safety or task success.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Sampling-based predictive control (SBPC) has been gaining traction in the nonlinear optimal control community. Completely general optimal control approaches often suffer from computational complexity associated with solving stochastic Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman equations backwards in time, commonly referred to as the curse of dimensionality. SBPC is an alternative to gradient-based model predictive control (MPC), where a simple sampling procedure is used in place of a sophisticated nonlinear optimizer \cite{posa2014direct, kurtz2023inverse, aydinoglu2024consensus}. Algorithms in this family include model predictive path integral control (MPPI) \cite{williams2016aggressive}, predictive sampling \cite{howell2022predictive}, and cross-entropy methods \cite{rubinstein1999cross}. MPPI, for example, circumvents computational complexity by employing forward sampling of stochastic dynamics combined with Monte Carlo approximation techniques \cite{williams2017model}. The optimal control is then computed as the exponentially weighted average of the sampled trajectories, enabling real-time implementation.
SBPC algorithms are particularly well-suited to recent advances in parallel computing architectures and parallel simulation \cite{mjx, Genesis, makoviychuk2021isaac}, enabling them to scale to complex problems like in-hand dexterous manipulation \cite{li2025drop}, legged locomotion \cite{xue2025full}, and more \cite{howell2022predictive}.

Despite these advantages, SBPCs such as MPPI inherently struggle with safety guarantees, such as collision avoidance.
Safety constraints are typically incorporated indirectly through penalty terms in the cost function by penalizing the collision~\cite{williams2017model, mohamed2023gp, yin2023shield}. However, relying solely on these penalties lacks explicit safety guarantees due to the nature of the sampling averaging procedure. 
The central technical question is therefore addressing whether the safety certificate survives the weighted averaging used by MPPI. This limitation motivates our goal to first integrate safety-critical constraints explicitly into the SBPC frameworks. We answer this question exactly for the executed next state under control-affine dynamics and for the entire predicted horizon under linear time-invariant dynamics.

A common method to ensure safety in collision avoidance tasks is to employ a safety filter, which minimally modifies the output of a potentially unsafe nominal controller to guarantee the satisfaction of the constraints \cite{ames2014cbf}. However, a simple safety filter applied \textit{only} to the control output is insufficient to guarantee safety due to the tight coupling between control objectives and long-term safety criteria that are potentially conflicting. Previous works have used control Lyapunov functions (CLF) and control barrier functions (CBF), often with relaxation factors, to solve this issue as an optimization problem, for mediating safety and feasibility \cite{ames2016control, zeng2021enhancing}.
Recent works have encoded this concept through predictive safety filters, which leverage predictive CBFs in layered control architectures \cite{compton2024learning}. However, in cluttered environments, this direct optimization often becomes non-convex and intractable.

This computational burden motivates a shift from online constrained optimization to learning-based control, where complex planning is performed offline to curate datasets of safe, multi-modal behavior. Generative models, such as diffusion and flow matching, can synthesize motion trajectories from diverse, high-dimensional demonstrations \cite{kurtz2025generative, chi2023diffusion}. However, they do not natively provide formal safety guarantees during rollout. 
Previous work attempts to encode safety by conditioning or regularizing generation \cite{dai2025safefm, carvalho2023mpdiffusion}, but deployments still lack mechanisms to systematically trade off safety and feasibility. This can be critical in real-world tasks where the robot must adjust caution to context. 
Recent works have incorporated MPC into refinement of generation to guarantee local optimality in its multi-modal behavior that is induced by reward-based guidance \cite{mizuta2025unified}. This allows generative methods to account for both safety and feasibility, but it can struggle to generalize to OOD environments, because this reward guidance can overly optimize the learned distribution \cite{dandapanthula2026we}.

A fundamental challenge in generative motion planning and control is addressing this distribution shift while maintaining safety and feasibility. Recent works suggest that self-generation using uncertainty-aware sampling in FM can be used to expand the generable set with probabilistic guarantee through an iterative verification and update process \cite{de2026active}. We extend this concept to a conditional flow matching (CFM) model, explicitly capturing the intrinsic trade-off between safety and feasibility through a verification process to generate a safe policy. To this end, we introduce the \textit{Safe Flow Expansion} framework, a novel approach for \textit{distilling safety} from a robust predictive planner into a generative policy.

\textbf{Contributions.} We make three contributions: (i) MPPI-DCBF, which rejects rollouts outside a common nominal polytope and preserves the executed next-state certificate under control-affine dynamics, and the full predicted certificate under LTI dynamics; (ii) a candidate-dependent SOCP verifier and uncertainty-guided Safe Flow Expansion procedure that adds verifier-positive OOD action windows to a CFM policy; and (iii) evaluations in 2D cluttered and pedestrian environments and a 3D quadrotor setting, including the effect of the safety parameter $\gamma$.

\section{Preliminaries}
\subsection{Model Predictive Path Integral control}
In discrete time, at state $x_k\in\mathbb R^{n_x}$, MPPI optimizes a mean control sequence $\mathbf u_{k:k+H-1|k}:=\{u_{k+i|k}\}_{i=0}^{H-1}$, where $H$ is the planning horizon.

%In discrete time, MPPI solves the following non-convex optimization problem at time step $k$ and state $\mathbf{x}(0) \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x}$ over the mean control sequence $\{ \mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}\}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_u}$ for $i \in  \{0, ..., N-1\}$
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:mppi}
\begin{align}
\min_{\{\mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}\}}
& \mathbb{E}\!\left[
\phi\!\left(\mathbf{x}_{k+N\mid k}\right)
\!+\!\!\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big(q(\mathbf{x}_{k+i\mid k})\!+\!\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}^\top R\,\mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}\Big)
\right], \label{eq:mppi-cost} \tag{1a}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
& \mathbf{x}_{k+i+1\mid k}=f\!\left(\mathbf{x}_{k+i\mid k},\,\mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}+\varepsilon_{k+i}\right), 
\label{eq:mppi-dyn}  \tag{1b}\\
& \mathbf{x}_{k+i\mid k} \in \mathcal{X}, \quad \mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k} \in \mathcal{U}, \label{eq:mppi-set1} \tag{1c} \\
& \mathbf{x}_{k\mid k}=\mathbf{x}_k, \quad \mathbf{x}_{k+H-1\mid k} \in \mathcal{X}_f,\qquad \label{eq:mppi-set2} \tag{1d}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Here, $N$ is the $q: \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is any running cost, $\phi : \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is a terminal cost, $\varepsilon_{k+i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\,\Sigma)$ is the control disturbance, $\Sigma$ is the control disturbance variance, $R \in \mathbb{R}^{n_u \times n_u}$ is the control cost weight.
% For the collision avoidance task, the controller can reason about crashing by setting collision penalties using the signed clearance to the closest obstacle $d_{\min}(\mathbf{x})$ in their running cost function, such as
% \begin{equation}
% \begin{aligned}
% q(\mathbf{x})
% &=(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{goal}})^\top Q (\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{goal}})\\
% &\quad+\kappa_{\text{soft}}\,e^{-\alpha\,d_{\min}(\mathbf{x})}
% +\kappa_{\text{hard}}\,\mathbf{1}\!\bigl[d_{\min}(\mathbf{x})<0\bigr].
% \label{eq:mppisoftconstraint}
% \end{aligned}
% \end{equation}
The MPPI optimal control sequence is a path-integral approximation of reward-weighted average random variations, which is solved by Monte Carlo methods, centered on the optimal policy sequence at the previous time step \cite{williams2017model}. The unexecuted part of the sequence is shifted by one time step and acts as the warm start of the next optimization. 

Let this warm start sequence from the previous time step be denoted by $\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{k+i\mid k}$. For rollout $m=1,\dots,M$ with perturbations $\varepsilon^{(m)}_{k+i}\!\sim\!\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)$, MPPI measures cost per rollout via forward sampling of random controls and states that can be expressed as
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
\mathbf{u}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}&=\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{k+i\mid k}+\varepsilon^{(m)}_{k+i},\label{eq:mppi_rollout}\\
\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+i+1\mid k}&=f\!\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k},\,\mathbf{u}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}\bigr).
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Then, the cost for each rollout is
\begin{equation}
S^{(m)}\!=\!\phi\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+N\mid k}\bigr)
+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big(q\,\!\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}\bigr)+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{u}^{(m)\top}_{k+i\mid k} R\,\mathbf{u}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}\Big).
\label{eq:mppicost}
\end{equation}
Information-theoretic MPPI uses soft-min importance weights \cite{williams2017info}:
\begin{equation}
\omega_m:=\exp\!\left(-\frac{S^{(m)}-S_{\min}}{\lambda_{\mathrm M}}\right),\qquad S_{\min}:=\min_{\ell=1,\ldots,M}S^{(\ell)}.
\label{eq:mppi-weights}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_M >0$ is a temperature which controls how heavily the optimal trajectory is weighted, and $S_\text{min}$ is the minimum rollout cost, used to improve the numerical stability. Then, for $i \in \{0, ..., N-1\}$, the normalized weights define the following Monte Carlo update of the mean control sequence:
\begin{equation}
\Delta \mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}=
\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{M}\omega_m\,\varepsilon^{(m)}_{k+i}}{\sum_{m=1}^{M}\omega_m},
\quad
\mathbf{u}^*_{k+i\mid k}\leftarrow \bar{\mathbf{u}}_{k+i\mid k}+\Delta \mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}. \label{eq:mppi-update}
\end{equation}
Then the current optimal control $\mathbf{u}^*_{k\mid k}$ is applied. 
% & h^{\mathrm{aff}}\!\left(x_{k+i\mid k};\,x_{k\mid k}\right)\ \ge\ (1-\gamma)^i,\quad i=0,\dots,N. \tag{1d}\label{eq:safemppi}
This parallelizable sampling process makes MPPI highly suitable for solving non-convex optimal control problems on modern GPU hardware. However, a key challenge arises in safety-critical applications. When obstacles are present, a cost for avoiding obstacles does not provide formal safety guarantees, as the final control is averaged over many trajectories. 

Several works have focused on incorporating safety into the forward sampling process. Approaches that shape the rollout distribution or impose barrier constraints \cite{yin2022,yin2023shield} bias the sampling toward safety, but often lack a safety certificate. Conversely, methods that act as sampling-based safety filters by greedily selecting safe samples \cite{rabiee2024guaranteed} enforce safety but bypass the averaging process in \eqref{eq:mppi-update}, thereby sacrificing the optimality certificate.

\subsection{Conditional Flow Matching for trajectory generation}
Conditional Flow matching is increasingly used in robotics for trajectory generation and policy generation \cite{kurtz2025generative,mizuta2025unified}. The trajectory generation process learns a mapping from noise to entire collision-free trajectories by end-to-end supervised learning, but it can struggle to generalize to out-of-distribution environments and may require complete retraining \cite{ye2024efficient, dai2025safefm}. 
% In contrast, (conditional) flow matching-based policy generation is more flexible, focuses on learning a mapping to a lower-dimensional control action, conditioned on observations \cite{kurtz2025generative}.

Here, we describe the important concept of conditional flow matching. For collision avoidance, it is essential to generate a trajectory conditioned on geometric observations while benefiting from offline flow matching training and fast computation from SBPC. To achieve this, the generative model predicts actions conditioned on the given observation $\mathbf{o_t}$ and executes for a short horizon \cite{chi2023diffusion}. 

%Concretely, at the current discrete time step $k$, the model takes $O$ steps of observations $\mathbf{o}_{k:k+O-1}$, predicts $H$ steps of actions $\mathbf{\hat u}_{k:k+H-1}$, execute $A$ steps of predicted actions $\mathbf{\hat u}_{k:k+A-1}$. Here, $O$ is the observation horizon, $H$ is the planning horizon, $A$ is the execution horizon of the action. Throughout our experiments, we used fixed values of $O = 1, H = 10, A = 1$. 

At planning step $k$, let $\xi_k$ denote the available planning context, including the current state or state estimate, goal, and sensed obstacle geometry. The policy conditions on the observation history $o_{k-O+1:k}\subset\xi_k$, generates the action window $\hat{\mathbf u}_k:=\hat u_{k:k+H-1}\in\mathcal U^H\subset\mathbb R^{Hn_u}$, and executes its first $A$ actions before replanning; throughout the experiments, $O=1$, $H=10$, and $A=1$. The CFM model learns the distribution of expert action windows, so we write $z:=\mathbf u^\star_{k:k+H-1|k}\in\mathbb R^{Hn_u}$ rather than identifying $z$ with the single action $u^\star_{k|k}$.

It is important to note that our framework utilizes a safety-critical MPPI planner as a pre-trained model and \textit{distills its safety} to a conditional flow matching. Here, $\mathbf{v}_t(\cdot)$ denotes the flow matching vector field that is learned to regress the behavior of the planner. We use \(\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_u}\) for a target optimal control policy, \textit{i.e.,} the symbol \(\mathbf{u}^*_{k\mid k}\) used in the previous section.

Conditional Flow matching aims to learn a marginal vector field $\mathbf{v}^*_t(x)$ that converts a simple distribution $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)$ into a complex data distribution $z \sim p_{data}$. This is done by considering the marginal probability path $p_t(x)$ that evolves from $\mathcal{N}(0, I_d)$ to $p_{data}$. With an optimal transport probability path that drives noise $\epsilon$ to $\mathbf{z}$ with constant direction and speed in time~\cite{lipman2022flow}, the conditional flow matching (CFM) model $\mathbf{v}_t^\theta(x)$ learns the conditional vector field $\mathbf{v}^*_t(x|\mathbf{z})$ per sampled point $x$ from conditional vector field by minimizing the mean-squared loss. :
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}(\theta)&=
\mathbb{E}_{\substack{
t\sim \mathrm{Unif}[0,1],\\
\mathbf{z}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}},\\ \mathbf{x}\sim p_t(\cdot\mid z)
}} \notag
\!\Bigl[\bigl\|\mathbf{v}_t^\theta(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{v}_t^*(\mathbf{x}\mid \mathbf{z})\bigr\|^2\Bigr]
\label{eq:cfm}\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{\substack{
t\sim \mathrm{Unif}[0,1],\\ \epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_d),\\
\mathbf{z}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}
}}
\!\Bigl[\bigl\|\mathbf{v}_t^\theta(t\mathbf{z}+(1-t)\epsilon)-(\mathbf{z}-\epsilon)\bigr\|^2\Bigr].
\end{align}

To generate a policy using the CFM model, we first assume there is a ground-truth marginal vector field that transforms noise into an optimal policy given observations. In our case, we view the task of generating safe robot motion trajectories conditioned on local geometric observations and states $\mathbf{o}$, a safety parameter $\gamma$. In this case, we need to expand the expectation from \eqref{eq:cfm} over all possible $o$, $\gamma$, and $c$. Thus, we define our training objective as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta)\!=\!
\mathbb{E}_{\substack{
t\sim \mathrm{Unif}[0,1],\\ \epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_d),\\
(z,o,\gamma)\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(z,o,\gamma)
}}
\!\!\!\!\Bigl[\bigl\|\mathbf{v}_t^\theta(tz+(1-t)\epsilon\!\mid\! o,\gamma)-(z-\epsilon)\bigr\|^2\Bigr].
\label{eq:cfm_cost}
\end{equation}

% Safe generative action sequence formulation should prompt temporal behavior and smoothness by receding horizon control \cite{chi2023diffusion}, similarly discrete-time barrier function constraints can be used to ensure safety \cite{zeng2021safety}.

% Flow matching aims to learn a marginal vector field $\textbf{v}^*_t(x)$ that converts a simple distribution $x_0 \sim p_0$, such as a standard isotropic Gaussian, into a complex data distribution $z \sim p_{data}$. This is done by considering the marginal probability path $p_t(x)$ that evolves from $p_0$ to $p_{data}$. Then, by simulating an ordinary differential equation (ODE) characterized by a marginal vector field, we can generate a meaningful sample.

\subsection{Discrete-Time Control Barrier Functions}
Let $\mathcal{C}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^n:\; h(x)\ge 0\}$ denote the safe set for a continuously differentiable $h$.
For the discrete‐time control‐affine system
\begin{equation}
    x_{k+1}=f(x_k)+g(x_k)u_k, \label{eq:affinesystem}
\end{equation}
a function $h$ is a discrete‐time control barrier function (DCBF) associated with the control system \eqref{eq:affinesystem} if there exists $\gamma\in(0,1]$ such that for all $x_k$ there exists a control $u_k$ with
\begin{equation}
    \Delta h(x_k,u_k)\;:=\;h(x_{k+1})-h(x_k)\;\ge\;-\gamma\,h(x_k).
    \label{eq:dtcbf}
\end{equation}
Iterating~\eqref{eq:dtcbf} yields $h(x_{k+i})\ge(1-\gamma)^i h(x_k)$ for every $i\ge0$; hence $h(x_k)\ge0$ implies discrete-time forward invariance. In the sequel, we enforce this DCBF-induced exponential envelope along the prediction horizon. This envelope is sufficient for predicted safe-set membership, but it is not equivalent to imposing (9) at every predicted transition.

%
% Condition \eqref{eq:dtcbf} implies the one‐step decay bound
% \begin{equation*}
%     h(x_{k+1})\;\ge\;(1-\gamma)\,h(x_k)
%     \, \Longrightarrow \,
%     h(x_k)\;\ge\;(1-\gamma)^{k}\,h(x_0),
%     \label{eq:exp_decay}
% \end{equation*}
% \textit{i.e.,} the lower bound on the barrier value decays exponentially with base $(1-\gamma)$, ensuring forward invariance of $\mathcal{C}$ \cite{ames2014cbf,agrawal2017discrete}.

\begin{remark} In order to prove $h$ is a valid DCBF, it is necessary to find a point-wise set of control values at any given state that is non-empty, with corresponding $\gamma$. To be compatible with the averaging in MPPI, we assumed that all rollouts \eqref{eq:mppi_rollout} assigned nonzero MPPI weight after rejection based on \eqref{eq:dtcbf}. In practice, we only used feasible sets of actions as data to train the generative policy.
\end{remark}

We define the safety specification that is derived from a supporting hyperplane to the single obstacle. Let \(\piq:\mathbb R^{n_x} \to \mathbb R^d\) be the projection that maps robot states into the robot position. \(\piq(\xki)=\qki\in\mathbb R^d\) denote the robot position at state \(\mathbf{x}_{k+i \mid k}\) at discrete time step $k$ and horizon $i = 0, ..., H-1$. For a convex obstacle, let $\mathbf{p}$ be the point on its boundary closest to the current position $\qk$, with outward normal $\mathbf{n}$. We define the barrier function as
\begin{equation}
h^{\mathrm{aff}}(\x;\xk) :=
\frac{\mathbf{n}^\top(\q-\p)}{\mathbf{n}^\top(\mathbf{q}_{k\mid k}-\mathbf{p})}.
\label{eq:aff_barrier}
\end{equation}
The implicit safe set for each horizon $i$ is defined as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{aff}}_i=\{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \mid h^{\mathrm{aff}}(\x;\xk)\ge (1-\gamma)^i\},\label{eq:aff_safeset}
\end{equation}
is a half-space, which is convex. Iterating the condition \eqref{eq:dtcbf} from $i=0$ with the normalization $h^{\mathrm{aff}}(\xk;\xk)=1$ yields an exponentially decaying lower bound on the safety specification along the prediction horizon:
\begin{equation}
h^{\mathrm{aff}}(\xki;\xk) \ge (1-\gamma)^i. \quad i = 0, ..., H-1.
\label{eq:safetyspecification}
\end{equation}

\noindent \textbf{Nominal Polytope for MPPI safety.} We further define the notion of a \textit{nominal polytope} to generalize from above local safety definition to multiple obstacles. 
We assume that the robot can only sense obstacles within a finite radial distance $R$, and that the index of obstacles that intersect within that region at the current position is denoted by $\mathcal{O}(\xk)$. The barrier function is defined as the minimum of the affine barrier functions \eqref{eq:safetyspecification} over obstacles in the sensing region:
\begin{equation}
    h^{\text{poly}}(\x;\xk) = \min_{j\in\mathcal{O}_j} h^{\mathrm{aff}}_j(\x;\x_k)
\end{equation}
The implicit safe set for each horizon $i=0,\cdots,H-1$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}^{\text{poly}}_i=\{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \mid h^{\text{poly}}(\x;\xk)\ge (1-\gamma)^i\},\label{eq:poly_safeset}
\end{equation}

Iterating this over nearby sensed obstacles yields convex polytope. The construction keeps the predicted discrete-time robot positions inside a conservative intersection of obstacle-separating half-spaces. Collision avoidance for a finite-size robot follows after configuration-space obstacle inflation, subject to the sensing, model, and intersample assumptions stated below.

\section{Problem Definition}

To achieve safety-critical policy generation, we first formulate our safe planning objective as a constrained optimization problem. Under pointwise feasibility and exact model, the executed next state satisfies the local nominal DCBF condition:
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:sc-mppi}
\begin{align*}
\min_{\{\mathbf{u}_{k:k+H-1\mid k}\}}
& \mathbb{E}\!\left[J(\mathbf{x}_{k}, \mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k})
\right], \tag{1a} \label{eq:mppi-dcbf}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
& \eqref{eq:mppi-dyn}, \ \eqref{eq:mppi-set1}, \ \eqref{eq:mppi-set2},\\
& h^{\text{poly}}(\xki;\xk)\ge (1-\gamma)^i. \tag{1e} \label{eq:poly}
\end{align*}
\end{subequations}

Direct optimization using a non-convex control barrier function that replaces $h^\text{poly}$ reduces to the problem MPC-CBF in the previous work \cite{zeng2021safety}. Despite the recursive feasibility of both problems not being generally guaranteed, from the view of set invariance, the DCBF decay,  $\gamma$, modulates the trade-off between \textit{safety and local feasibility}. For example, small $\gamma$ yields higher value of barrier function as can be directly seen from~\eqref{eq:poly}, but is less feasible.  
% (Here say something about their optimization problem's downside e.g., sensitive to initialization and struggle in dynamic settings...) 
However, the non-convex nature of the resulting optimization problem on arbitrary $J(\mathbf{x}_{k}, \mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k})$ often makes real-time implementation computationally intractable \cite{rawlings2020model}. SBPC methods such as MPPI face similar challenges, and often mitigate pointwise infeasibility by shaping the sampling distribution \cite{yin2022, yin2023shield}, but this trades-off safety and optimality. 

% However, in practice, constructing bCBFs in a task-agnostic way is challenging. 

MPPI-DCBF provides a model-based source of nominal-polytope-certified behavior. However, the nominal polytope is intentionally conservative: all contributing rollouts must share the same convex safe set so that their MPPI-weighted average remains safe. Moreover, repeatedly invoking MPPI-DCBF to cover every shifted environment is computationally expensive. A CFM policy amortizes this planner, but it assigns substantial probability only to behaviors represented in its demonstration distribution. Consequently, in a shifted context, the probability of sampling a verifier-positive action window may be small.

This gap motivates Safe Flow Expansion. At target contexts, the current CFM proposes candidate action windows, a candidate-dependent verifier certifies those satisfying the finite-horizon DCBF decay condition, and the accepted windows are used to expand the policy beyond its original support. Our objective is therefore to increase the probability of generating a certified action window while retaining the safety--feasibility conditioning parameter $\gamma$.

We use OOD relative to the CFM pretraining distribution. Let $p_{\mathrm{pre}}(\xi)$ denote the distribution of planning contexts induced by the environments, initial and goal states, and obstacle motions used to collect the MPPI-DCBF demonstrations. We call a context \emph{pretraining-OOD} when it is drawn from a held-out target distribution $p_{\mathrm{tgt}}(\xi)$ whose environment parameters were not used for expert-data collection or CFM pretraining. Here, $\xi$ denotes the planning context available to the policy, including the current state or state estimate, the goal, and the sensed obstacle geometry. The safety parameter $\gamma$ is treated separately as a
conditioning variable.

% We consider a robot whose dynamics are described by the discrete-time control affine system in \eqref{eq:affinesystem}, evolving in a cluttered environment with static obstacles. This work has two primary objectives. First, we experimentally demonstrate that our proposed controller, MPPI-DCBF, achieves superior safety guarantees over a standard baseline MPPI with different choices of the safety parameter $\gamma$. We validate this approach and its scalability on both LTI systems and complex nonlinear models, such as a quadrotor. Second, we address the challenge of creating a real-time generative policy that can modulate the trade-off between safety and feasibility by continuously scheduling $\gamma$, based on observations.

\section{Method}

Starting from conservative MPPI-DCBF demonstrations, we use candidate-dependent verification to discover additional action windows that satisfy the same finite-horizon DCBF decay condition but need not remain inside the nominal expert polytope.

\subsection{The MPPI-DCBF Algorithm}

We first propose model predictive path integral control with DCBF-based sample rejection (MPPI-DCBF), which modifies the standard MPPI framework to formally enforce safety. The core of our method is the safety specification in \eqref{eq:poly}, which is designed to guarantee the forward invariance of the sequence of time-varying safe sets $\mathcal{C}_i^{\mathrm{poly}}$ defined in \eqref{eq:poly_safeset}. This is achieved by integrating the DCBF condition directly into the sampling step of the MPPI algorithm. Any sampled rollout that violates the condition is rejected; consequently, the optimal control sequence \eqref{eq:mppi-update} is computed as a convex combination of only those rollouts that are certified to be safe. {\color{red} MH Question: Will MPPI still converge to the optimal policy after rejecting the samples?} Under the assumption of pointwise feasibility\footnote{For the discrete-time control affine system \eqref{eq:affinesystem} and for any initial state $x_{k|k} \in \mathcal{C}_i^\mathrm{poly}$, there exists at least one control sequence $\{u_{k+i|k}\}_{i=0}^{N-1}$ such that the resulting trajectory $\{x_{k+i|k}\}_{i=1}^{N}$ lies within the sequence of safe sets defined in \eqref{eq:poly_safeset}, i.e., $x_{k+i|k} \in \mathcal{C}_i^{\mathrm{poly}}$ for all $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$.

\subsection{Multi-step Safety Verification}
Before presenting Safe Flow Expansion to bridge the gap between generative policy and the SBPC, we define a deterministic verifier that tests whether a candidate action window admits a finite-horizon DCBF certificate, (\textit{i.e.,} multi-step safety).

\noindent \textbf{Verifier Polytope for Expansion.}
The nominal polytope in \eqref{eq:poly_safeset} is a convex inner approximation imposed on all nonzero-weight MPPI rollouts, thereby enabling the convexity-based averaging argument. In a nonconvex free space, however, a single shared region may exclude collision-free rollouts in other local modes, even if that region is enlarged~\cite{deits2015computing}. Therefore, to expand a generative policy into these unexplored safe modes, it is important to preserve the finite-horizon DCBF decay schedule while reducing conservatism of the convex inner approximation from the nominal polytope. Given an action window proposed by the generative policy, the verifier instead evaluates its induced rollout individually at the current receding-horizon step, allowing the separating faces to depend on the queried rollout. 

For a queried action window \(\hat{\mathbf u}\), let
\(\{\xki\}_{i=0}^{H}\) denote its model rollout. For each sensed obstacle \(j\in\mathcal O(\xk)\), with center $\oj$ and radius
\(r_j\), the verifier constructs a separating hyperplane \(\mathbf a_j^\top(\mathbf q-\qk)\le m_j,\)
by solving the optimization problem
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\max_{\aj, m_j}\ & m_j\\
\text{s.t. }\ & \aj^\top(\qki-c)\le\big(1-(1-\gamma)^t\big)m_i \quad (i=0,\dots,H-1),\\
& r_j\|\aj\|_2\le \aj^\top(\oj-c)-m_i,\\
&\|\aj\|_2\le1,\ m_j\ge m_{\min}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:socp}
\end{equation}
% The objective is linear, the trajectory constraints are affine, and the obstacle-separation and normalization constraints are second-order-cone representable; hence the problem is an SOCP. 
Here, the first constraint is the multi-step safety specification
\(h^{\text{poly}}(\xki;\xk)\geq \alpha_t\) and the second constraint is the separation since

% \(\min_{x\in\mathcal O_i}\ai^\top(x-c)
% =\aj^\top(o_j-c_j)-r_j\|\aj\|_2\). 

The third and fourth constraints are the normalization bound and the minimum margin specification, respectively.
% {\color{red}MJ: Need more detailed explanation on this optimization problem.}
After solving all per-obstacle faces, the verifier polytope has the form \footnote{In order to guarantee the bounded verifier polytope in the sensing region, we solve \eqref{eq:socp} for auxiliary obstacles that is tangent to the largest K-sided polytope; K = 16 is used.}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P
=
\bigcap_j
\{x:\aj^\top(x-c_j)\le m_j\},
\label{eq:verifierpolytope}
\end{equation}
% or equivalently \(Ax\le b\) with \(A_i=a_i^\top\) and
% \(b_i=a_i^\top c+m_i\). 
Having formulated the optimization problem for the existence of multi-step safety specification, we formally define the \textit{validity} of an arbitrary control policy. Here we abbreviate the \(H\) steps of predicted actions
\(\hat{\mathbf u}_{k:k+H-1}\) as \(\hat{\mathbf u}\).
\begin{definition}
Let \(v_\gamma(\hat{\mathbf u};\xi) \in \{0, 1\}\) be a indicator function that returns \(1\) if the optimization problem~\eqref{eq:socp} is feasible for action \(\hat{\mathbf u}\) under context \(\xi\) and safety parameter \(\gamma\), and \(0\) otherwise. The \textit{valid action set} is defined as the action certified multi-step safety of~\eqref{eq:socp}
\begin{equation}
    \Omega^\star(\xi,\gamma)
    :=
    \left\{
    \hat{\mathbf u}\in\mathcal U^H:
    v_\gamma(\hat{\mathbf u};\xi)=1
    \right\},
    \label{eq:verifier-certified-set}
\end{equation}
and action \(\hat{\mathbf u} \in \Omega^\star(\xi,\gamma)\) is a \textit{valid} action that consequently  guarantees the existence of a verifier polytope~\eqref{eq:verifierpolytope}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition} \label{prop: 3} A stochastic policy $\mathbf{u}_{k:k+H-1\mid k}$ with length $H$ produced by MPPI-DCBF~\eqref{eq:mppi-dcbf} associated with safety parameter $\gamma$, which is the constrained stochastic optimization problem solution under the outward normal $n$ from \eqref{eq:aff_barrier}, is a valid multi-step sample, \textit{i.e.,} \(v_\gamma(\mathbf{u}_{k:k+H-1\mid k};\xi) =1.\) Therefore, the nominal-polytope certificate induces a feasible face of the SOCP verifier.
\end{proposition}

Generative policies constrain actions to the distribution of their demonstrations.
While Proposition~\ref{prop: 3} formally guarantees that the MPPI-DCBF planner allows us to produce rapid safe demonstrations as verifier-positive trajectories, relying solely on these demonstrations inherently yields a subset of the distribution of all possible safe actions.

\subsection{Safe Flow Expansion}
We now turn our attention to acquiring missing safe behavioral modes for a conditional generative policy. Safe Flow Expansion enables this by first pretraining from that safe action support and increasing the probability mass on the same multi-step safe action windows.

 This becomes problematic in OOD scenarios, where the model encounters states unseen during training. To address this distribution shift, recent large-scale imitation learning frameworks attempt to gather massive amounts of human intervention data to fill the coverage gaps \cite{}. However, data collection is inherently unscalable for capturing all possible safety-critical actions. To this end, we instead use the current generative policy to propose candidate action windows, query their validity with the deterministic verifier, and retrain on the resulting verified data~\cite{de2026active}. 

Concretely, for a given state-observation context \(\xi\) and safety parameter \(\gamma\), the policy at expansion round \(n\) induces the \textit{conditional generable set} associated with the probability density lower bound \(\tau\) defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_{\theta_n}^{\tau}(\xi,\gamma)
    :=
    \left\{
    \hat{\mathbf u}\in\mathcal U^H:
    p_1^{\theta_n}(\hat{\mathbf u}\mid\xi,\gamma)\ge\tau
    \right\}.
    \label{eq:conditional-generable-set}
\end{equation}
Here, the pre-trained generable set is recovered by setting \(n=0\). The conditional generable set represents the actions the policy is likely given $\tau$, whereas the valid action set denotes the actions formally certified as safe. Here, by Proposition~\ref{prop: 3}, every MPPI-DCBF receding horizon belongs to \(\Omega^\star(\xi,\gamma)\).

Algorithm~\ref{alg:sfe} implements data acquisition on verified actions under the evolving CFM policy. At each receding-horizon step, the current policy proposes uncertainty-tilted actions, and among those, the verifier \(v_{\gamma_k}\) is used to acquire safe actions. The data is collected in a receding-horizon manner, provided that \(\xi_k\) is a sufficient statistic for the system dynamics (\textit{i.e.,} input of the generative policy), this receding-horizon interaction induces a Markovian closed-loop sampling process. During the data acquisition, one can execute (the execution cost \(C\)) expert actions \cite{ross2011reduction} or restrict execution to a safe admissible action set \cite{alshiekh2018safe}. In our case, verifier validity remains enforced by restricting selection to \(\mathcal J_k^+\) and constructing this execution cost for efficient safe coverage remains an open question. 


\noindent \textbf{Baseline method} We compared against with CFM-MPPI~\cite{mizuta2025unified}, which combines reward-guided flow generation with sampling-based MPPI refinement. Given a base pre-trained vector field $\mathbf{v}^{\theta_{\text{0}}}(\mathbf{x}\!\mid\! \mathbf{o},\gamma)$, the guided velocity field is the local gradient ascent of the combined reward:
\begin{equation}
   \tilde{\mathbf{v}}^{\theta_{\text{0}}}(\mathbf{x}_\tau\!\mid\! \mathbf{o},\gamma)  = \mathbf{v}^{\theta_{\text{0}}}(\mathbf{x}_\tau\!\mid\! \mathbf{o},\gamma) \;+\; \eta \nabla_{\mathbf{z}_1}R(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z}_1),
   \label{eq:cfm-mppi_vectorfield}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{\hat u} =\mathbf{z}_1 \sim p_1^{\theta_{\mathrm{0}}}(\cdot\mid\mathbf
o,\gamma)$ By integrating over $\tau$ from 0 to 1, the guided policy locally increases the reward that is designed to encourage both performance and safety
\begin{equation}
    R(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\hat u}) = \sum_{k=0}^{H-1} w_\text{s} r_\text{s}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\hat u}) + w_\text{g} r_\text{g} (\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\hat u}), 
    \label{eq:cfm-mppi_guidance}
\end{equation}
where $w_\text{s}, w_\text{g}$ are scalar weights. The safety reward term $r_\text{s}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\hat u})$ is defined to discourage the CBF condition, such as in \cite[Eq. 6]{mizuta2025unified}. While can be set arbitrarily, we set the performance reward term $r_\text{g}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\hat u})$ to be the same cost of the MPPI-DCBF expert \eqref{eq:mppicost}. In our simulation studies, we evaluated various combinations of the scalar weights and selected the pair that provided the best trade-off between safety (collision rate) and task performance (time-to-goal).

At every receding-horizon step, CFM-MPPI generates 200 guided control
sequences of horizon $H=10$ from the pretrained model. The ten sequences
with the lowest MPPI cost are retained, and each is perturbed 200
times with Gaussian noise. MPPI cost-weighted averaging produces ten
refined sequences, from which the minimum-cost sequence is selected and
its first action is executed. At the next state, the selected sequence is
shifted by one step and used as a warm-start at the flow time $\tau = 0.75$ to generate the
next guided generation and refinement step.

\section{Conclusion}

Future work: extend to the systems where expert demonstration is expensive or task / state space is too large.

% This paper presented a framework for creating safety-tunable generative control policies for safety-critical tasks. We introduced a novel MPPI-DCBF planner that uses DCBFs to generate a rich dataset of provably safe multi-modal trajectories for linear systems. By training a conditional flow matching model on this data, we produced a real-time policy that can modulate its safety and feasibility via a single parameter at inference time, without the need for online sampling. Our experiments on 2D and 3D systems validate the approach, demonstrating a practical method for deploying safe, adaptable, and high-performance learned controllers.
