\begin{abstract}
Sampling-based predictive control (SBPC) (e.g. model predictive path integral control (MPPI)) efficiently predicts receding-horizon actions.  However, the safety of individual MPPI rollouts do not guarantee a safe weighted average.  Moreover, policies pretrained on safe demonstrations may poorly represent safe behaviors in out-of-distribution (OOD) deployments. This paper introduces new methods to ensure safety in SBPC and to guarantee a conditional lower bound on the probability of generating a safe action.
We address sampling-based safety through a discrete-time control barrier function (DCBF) condition, enforced conservatively to produce certified safe data and to verify new behaviors.
We first introduce MPPI-DCBF, which rejects unsafe samples within a convex safe region before averaging and preserves the executed next-state safety certificate under control-affine dynamics and the full predicted-horizon certificate for linear time-invariant systems. 
Building on this certified planner, we introduce an offline framework, \textit{Safe Flow Expansion}, that iteratively expands the range of safe actions of the learned Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) model through uncertainty-guided self-generation, verification, and update.
In unseen 2D and 3D environments, we show that our framework improves safety and mode coverage with much lower replanning latency than baseline methods, while tuning safety levels via a single DCBF parameter.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% \note{I think we need to stress that MPPI-DCBF can offer an on-line safety procedure in addition to off-line training data.}
%%%%%% dropped citations: kurtz2023inverse, aydinoglu2024consensus, Genesis, williams2017model, williams2017info, nguyen2025flowmp, ye2024efficient, mohamed2023gp

Sampling-based predictive control (SBPC) is gaining traction as an optimal control framework. Completely general optimal control approaches often suffer from the computational complexity that is associated with solving stochastic Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman equations backward in time. SBPC is an alternative to gradient-based model predictive control (MPC), where a simple sampling procedure replaces a sophisticated nonlinear optimizer~\cite{posa2014direct}. 
Algorithms in this family include model predictive path integral control (MPPI)~\cite{williams2016aggressive}, predictive sampling~\cite{howell2022predictive}, Generative Predictive Control~\cite{Kurtz_GPC}, and cross-entropy methods~\cite{rubinstein1999cross}. MPPI, for example, circumvents computational complexity by employing forward sampling of stochastic dynamics combined with Monte Carlo approximation techniques~\cite{williams2016aggressive}. 
% The optimal control is then computed as the exponentially weighted average of the sampled trajectories, enabling real-time implementation.
SBPC algorithms are particularly well-suited to recent advances in parallel computing architectures~\cite{mjx, makoviychuk2021isaac}, enabling them to scale to complex problems like in-hand dexterous manipulation~\cite{li2025drop}, legged locomotion~\cite{xue2025full}, and more~\cite{howell2022predictive, Kurtz_GPC}.

Despite these advantages, SBPCs such as MPPI, inherently struggle with safety guarantees. Safety constraints are typically incorporated indirectly through penalty terms in the path planning cost function~\cite{williams2017model, yin2023shield}. While this approach encourages safe behavior, it lacks explicit safety guarantees because the procedure relies on sampling and averaging. 
% For example, the failure in Fig.~\ref{fig:hero} occurs when rollouts from different local safe regions are averaged (\textit{e.g.}, left and right mode).
Even if individual sampled trajectories satisfy safety constraints, their weighted average may not. This limitation, 
%which applies to all current SBPC methods, 
motivates our goal of first integrating safety-critical constraints explicitly into SBPC frameworks. To this end, we introduce MPPI-DCBFs, which prevent such failure modes by restricting all nonzero-weight rollouts to a common convex nominal polytope before averaging.

A common method to ensure safety in collision-avoidance tasks is to impose finite-horizon CBF constraints directly within MPC~\cite{zeng2021enhancing}, under nonlinear dynamics and nonconvex obstacle geometry. However, the resulting optimization can be nonconvex and computationally heavy. This computational burden motivates a shift from online constrained optimization to learning-based methods, where complex planning is performed offline to curate datasets of safe and multi-modal behavior. Generative models, such as diffusion and flow matching (FM)~\cite{lipman2022flow}, can synthesize motion trajectories and action sequences from diverse, high-dimensional demonstrations~\cite{chi2023diffusion, Kurtz_GPC}. Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) has emerged as a promising alternative to diffusion-based modeling, offering competitive generation quality with efficient sampling~\cite{chisari2024learning, ye2024efficient}. 
% CFM is particularly useful in safety-critical motion planning and control, as it can generate high-fidelity, multi-modal trajectories with fewer inference time steps. Maintaining this topological diversity is crucial to avoid committing to a single local solution that may become infeasible as the scene changes~\cite{de2024topology, shafiullah2022behavior}. 
However, they do not natively provide formal safety guarantees during rollout. 

Recent CFM-based works have further incorporated explicit safety constraints at inference time by trajectory regularization or constrained generation~\cite{li2026hardflow,utkarsh2026physics}, while MPC-based refinement has been used to improve local optimality of generated trajectories~\cite{mizuta2025unified}. However, these methods guide or refine samples from a fixed pretrained distribution. In OOD contexts, the pretrained model may assign low probability to safe and multi-modal behaviors, and aggressive guidance can further push generation away from its learned distribution~\cite{dandapanthula2026we}.

Consequently, a fundamental challenge in generative motion planning and control is to address this distribution shift by expanding the generable set of a pretrained policy toward safe and multi-modal regions. Recent work formalizes this as generable set expansion~\cite{de2026active} to expand the range of behaviors the FM model can generate. We extend this concept to a CFM model to discover new safe behaviors in robotics motion planning. To this end, we introduce the \textit{Safe Flow Expansion} framework, which expands the pretrained policy toward new safe behaviors using a safety-critical predictive planner and verifier mechanisms. Under the stated safety conditions, MPPI-DCBF can be deployed as an online receding-horizon controller while also generating certified demonstrations for offline CFM training.

\textbf{Contributions.} Our contributions are threefold:
(i) A safety-tunable online MPPI-DCBF planner that rejects sampled rollouts violating finite-horizon DCBF conditions before weighted averaging, preserving the safety certificate of the executed next state under control-affine dynamics and the entire predicted horizon for LTI systems.
(ii) Safe Flow Expansion, an offline uncertainty-guided procedure that self-generates and verifies CFM actions in OOD environments, therefore expands the pretrained policy toward new safe behaviors. 
(iii) A closed-loop analysis and evaluation that carries the DCBF parameter of MPPI-DCBF through certified demonstration generation derives a conditional lower bound on probabilistic safety for a safety-tunable generative policy. Evaluations in 2D cluttered and pedestrian environments and on a 3D quadrotor show increased multi-step safe probability and recovery of safe trajectory modes in OOD environments.

\section{Preliminaries}
\subsection{Model Predictive Path Integral control}
In discrete time, at state $\xk\in\mathbb R^{n_x}$, MPPI optimizes a mean control sequence $\{ \uki \}_{i=0}^{H-1}$, where $H$ is the planning horizon.
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:mppi}
\begin{align}
\min_{\{\mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}\}}
& \mathbb{E}\!\left[
\phi\!\left(\mathbf{x}_{k+H\mid k}\right)
\!+\!\!\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}\Big(q(\mathbf{x}_{k+i\mid k})\!+\!\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}^\top R\,\mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}\Big)
\right], \label{eq:mppi-cost} \tag{1a}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
& \mathbf{x}_{k+i+1\mid k}=f\!\left(\mathbf{x}_{k+i\mid k},\,\mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}+\varepsilon_{k+i}\right), 
\label{eq:mppi-dyn}  \tag{1b}\\
& \mathbf{x}_{k+i\mid k} \in \mathcal{X}, \quad \mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k} \in \mathcal{U}, \label{eq:mppi-set1} \tag{1c} \\
& \mathbf{x}_{k\mid k}=\mathbf{x}_k, \quad \mathbf{x}_{k+H-1\mid k} \in \mathcal{X}_f,\qquad \label{eq:mppi-set2} \tag{1d}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Here, $q: \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is a running cost, $\phi : \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is a terminal cost, $\varepsilon_{k+i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\,\Sigma)$ is the control disturbance, $\Sigma$ is the control disturbance variance, $R \in \mathbb{R}^{n_u \times n_u}$ is the control cost weight.

The MPPI optimal control sequence is a path-integral approximation of reward-weighted average random variations, solved by Monte Carlo methods and centered on the optimal policy sequence from the previous time step \cite{williams2017model}. The unexecuted part of the sequence shifts by one time step and serves as the warm start for the next optimization. 

Let this warm start sequence from the previous time step be denoted by $\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{k+i\mid k}$. For rollout $m=1,\dots,M$ with perturbations $\varepsilon^{(m)}_{k+i}\!\sim\!\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)$, MPPI measures cost per rollout via forward sampling of random controls and states that can be expressed as
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
\mathbf{u}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}&=\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{k+i\mid k}+\varepsilon^{(m)}_{k+i},\label{eq:mppi_rollout}\\
\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+i+1\mid k}&=f\!\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k},\,\mathbf{u}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}\bigr).
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Then, the cost for each rollout is
\begin{equation}
S^{(m)}\!=\!\phi\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+H\mid k}\bigr)
+\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}\Big(q\,\!\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}\bigr)+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{u}^{(m)\top}_{k+i\mid k} R\,\mathbf{u}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}\Big),
\label{eq:mppicost}
\end{equation}
and soft-min importance weights:
\begin{equation}
\omega_m\!:=\!\exp(-(S^{(m)}\!\!-\!S_{\min})/{\lambda}),\; S_{\min}\!:=\!\!\!\min_{\ell=1,\ldots,M}S^{(\ell)}.
\label{eq:mppi-weights}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda>0$ is a temperature that controls how heavily the optimal trajectory is weighted, and $S_\text{min}$ is the minimum rollout cost, used to improve numerical stability. Then, for $i \in \{0, ..., H-1\}$, the normalized weights define the following Monte Carlo update of the mean control sequence:
\begin{equation}
\Delta \mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}=
\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{M}\omega_m\,\varepsilon^{(m)}_{k+i}}{\sum_{m=1}^{M}\omega_m},
\quad
\mathbf{u}^*_{k+i\mid k}\leftarrow \bar{\mathbf{u}}_{k+i\mid k}+\Delta \mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}. \label{eq:mppi-update}
\end{equation}
Then the current optimal control $\mathbf{u}^*_{k\mid k}$ is applied. 

This parallelizable sampling process makes MPPI well-suited to solving non-convex optimal control problems on modern GPU hardware. However, a key challenge arises in safety-critical applications. Prior work either biases the sampling distribution toward safety without a certificate~\cite{yin2022,yin2023shield}, or uses a cost-weighted MPPI update only to adapt the sampling distribution while executing the first input of the lowest-cost rollout under CBF filter~\cite{rabiee2024guaranteed}

% However, a key challenge arises in safety-critical applications. When obstacles are present, an obstacle-avoidance cost does not provide formal safety guarantees, as the final control is averaged over many trajectories. 

% Several works have focused on incorporating safety into the forward sampling process of MPPI. Approaches that shape the rollout distribution or impose barrier constraints to bias the sampling toward safety \cite{yin2022,yin2023shield} often lack a safety certificate. Conversely, methods that act as sampling-based safety filters by greedily selecting safe samples \cite{rabiee2024guaranteed} enforce safety but bypass the MPPI cost-weighted update~\eqref{eq:mppi-update}.

\subsection{Discrete-Time Control Barrier Functions}
Let $\mathcal{C}=\{\x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_x}:\; h(\x)\ge 0\}$ denote the safe set for a continuously differentiable $h$.
For the discrete‐time control‐affine system
\begin{equation}
    \x_{k+1}=f(\x_k)+g(\x_k)\u_k, \label{eq:affinesystem}
\end{equation}
A function $h$ is a discrete‐time control barrier function (DCBF) associated with the control system \eqref{eq:affinesystem} if there exists $\gamma\in(0,1]$ such that for all $\x_k$ there exists a control $\u_k$ with
\begin{equation}
    \Delta h(\x_k,\u_k)\;:=\;h(\x_{k+1})-h(\x_k)\;\ge\;-\gamma\,h(\x_k).
    \label{eq:dtcbf}
\end{equation}
Condition \eqref{eq:dtcbf} implies the one‐step decay bound
\begin{equation*}
    h(\x_{k+1})\;\ge\;(1-\gamma)\,h(\x_k)
    \, \Longrightarrow \,
    h(\x_k)\;\ge\;(1-\gamma)^{k}\,h(\x_0),
    \label{eq:exp_decay}
\end{equation*}
\textit{i.e.,} the lower bound on the barrier value decays exponentially with base $(1-\gamma)$, ensuring forward invariance of $\mathcal{C}$ \cite{ames2014cbf,agrawal2017discrete}.

We first define the safety specification derived from a supporting hyperplane to the single obstacle. Let \(\piq:\mathbb R^{n_x} \to \mathbb R^d\) be the projection that maps robot states into the robot position. \(\piq(\xki)=\qki\in\mathbb R^d\) denote the robot position at state \(\mathbf{x}_{k+i \mid k}\) at discrete time step $k$ and horizon $i = 1, ..., H$. For each convex configuration-space obstacle $j$, let $\p_j$ be the point on its boundary closest to the current position $\qk$, with outward normal $\n_j$. The affine barrier function is defined as
\setlength{\belowdisplayskip}{2pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayshortskip}{2pt}
\begin{equation}
h_j^{\mathrm{aff}}(\x;\xk) :=
\frac{\n_j^\top(\q-\p_j)}{\n_j^\top(\q_{k\mid k}-\mathbf{p_j})}.
\label{eq:aff_barrier}
\end{equation}
Since $\n_j^\top(\qk-\p_j)>0$, the safe set for each horizon $i$ can be defined as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{aff}}_{i,j}=\{\x \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \mid h_j^{\mathrm{aff}}(\x;\xk)\ge (1-\gamma)^i\},\label{eq:aff_safeset}
\end{equation}
is a convex half-space. Iterating the condition \eqref{eq:dtcbf} from $i=0$ with the normalization $h_j^{\mathrm{aff}}(\xk;\xk)=1$ yields
\begin{equation}
h_j^{\mathrm{aff}}(\xki;\xk) \ge (1-\gamma)^i. \quad i = 1, \ldots, H.
\label{eq:safetyspecification}
\end{equation}

\noindent \textbf{Nominal Polytope for MPPI safety.} We further define the notion of a \textit{nominal polytope} to generalize the safety definition to multiple obstacles. Let $\mathcal O(\xk)$ index the obstacles observed at the current robot state. We define the nominal ego-centric (\textit{i.e.}, defined as a function of $\xk$) safety function
\begin{equation}
    h^{\mathrm{poly}}(\x;\xk) = \min_{j\in\mathcal O(\xk)} h^{\mathrm{aff}}_j(\x;\x_{k\mid k})
\end{equation}
Accordingly, the safe set for each horizon,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{poly}}_i=\{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \mid h^{\mathrm{poly}}(\x;\xk)\ge (1-\gamma)^i\},\label{eq:poly_safeset}
\end{equation}
Thus, although the complete free space around multiple obstacles is generally nonconvex, $\mathcal C_i^{\mathrm{poly}}$ is a conservative convex inner approximation set obtained by intersecting half-spaces that separates the current robot position from the obstacles. Furthermore, it is constructed as a bounded set by incorporating bounding faces within a sensing range which will be discussed further.

While the proper design of terminal ingredients (\textit{i.e.,} a terminal set and cost) can formally guarantee recursive feasibility in traditional MPC, the non-convex nature of the resulting optimization problem often makes real-time implementation computationally intractable \cite{rawlings2020model}. SBPC methods such as MPPI face similar challenges. Rather than proving recursive feasibility, our approach ensures safety by enforcing DCBF constraints at each time step, which relies on the weaker but more practical condition of pointwise feasibility, formalized in the following assumption.

\begin{assumption}[Pointwise feasibility]
\label{assumption_1}
For the discrete-time control affine system \eqref{eq:affinesystem}, for every replanning state $\xk$, and DCBF parameter $\gamma \in (0,1]$, 
there exists a control sequence\footnote{Assumption~\ref{assumption_1} is the condition for pointwise feasibility of the MPPI-DCBF algorithm at the current state; it ensures that the set of DCBF-certified trajectories for the planner to find is non-empty.}
$\{ \uki \}_{i=0}^{H-1}\in\mathcal U^H$ such that the resulting trajectory $\{\xki\}_{i=1}^{H}$ lies within the sequence of safe sets defined in~\eqref{eq:poly_safeset}, i.e., $\xki \in \mathcal{C}_i^{\mathrm{poly}}$:
\begin{equation}
h^{\mathrm{poly}}(\xki;\xk)\ge(1-\gamma)^i,
\quad i=1,\ldots,H.\label{eq:nominalpolytope}
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\section{Problem Definition}
To generate safety-critical policies, we pose safe planning as a constrained
optimization problem. Let $h(\cdot;\xk)$ be any ego-centric safety function
whose nonnegative superlevel set represents the modeled safe states at
planning step $k$, held fixed over the prediction horizon and normalized by
$h(\xk;\xk)=1$. The formulation is \textit{agnostic} to this choice:

\setlength{\abovedisplayskip}{2pt}

\subsection{MPPI-DCBF}
We first propose model predictive path integral control with DCBF-based sample rejection (MPPI-DCBF), which modifies the standard MPPI framework to formally enforce safety. The core of our method is the safety specification in \eqref{eq:poly}, which is designed to guarantee the forward invariance of the sequence of time-varying safe sets $\mathcal{C}_i^{\mathrm{poly}}$ defined in \eqref{eq:poly_safeset}. This is achieved by integrating the DCBF condition directly into the MPPI sampling step. We reject any sampled rollout that violates the condition; consequently, we compute the optimal control sequence \eqref{eq:mppi-update} as a convex combination of only rollouts certified to be safe. Under the pointwise feasibility assumption (Assumption.~\ref{assumption_1}), we can formally analyze the properties of the MPPI-DCBF averaging process.

\begin{proposition} \label{prop:1}
    For the discrete-time control affine system \eqref{eq:affinesystem}, if all contributing rollouts $(m)$ satisfy $\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+1\mid k} \in \mathcal{C}_1^{\mathrm{poly}}$, then the averaged state $\mathbf{x}^*_{k+1|k}$ under the MPPI-DCBF control is also guaranteed to be in the safe set $\mathcal{C}_1^{\mathrm{poly}}$.
    
    % \begin{proof}
    % Under the control affine dynamics, the state $\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+1\mid k}$ in the next time step is an affine function of the control inputs. Since the admissible inputs are a convex polytope and the control update of MPPI is a convex combination of control perturbations~\eqref{eq:mppi-update}, the resulting state $\mathbf{x}^*_{k+1\mid k}$ is a convex combination of the rollout states:
    % \begin{align}
    % \mathbf{x}^*_{k+1|k} = \sum_m \hat{\omega}_m \mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+1|k}, \nonumber
    % \end{align}
    % where $\hat{\omega}_m \in \mathbb{R}_{> 0}$ and $\sum_m \hat{\omega}_m = 1$ are the importance weights derived from \eqref{eq:mppi-weights} after the rejection of unsafe samples. Moreover, since the safe set $\mathcal{C}_1^{\mathrm{poly}}$ is a convex set, any convex combination of points within it must also lie within the set. Thus, if $\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+1\mid k} \in \mathcal{C}_1^{\mathrm{poly}}$ for all $m$, it follows that $\mathbf{x}^*_{k+1|k} \in \mathcal{C}_1^{\mathrm{poly}}$.
    % \end{proof}
\end{proposition}

If the dynamics of the system of interest are linear time-invariant (LTI) as

\begin{proposition} \label{prop:2}
For the discrete LTI system \eqref{eq:lti} under the same pointwise feasibility assumption, if all contributing rollouts $(m)$ satisfy $\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k} \in \mathcal{C}_i^{\mathrm{poly}}$ for all steps $i \in \{1, \dots, H\}$, then the entire averaged MPPI trajectory also remains within the sequence of safe sets, \textit{i.e.,} $\mathbf{x}^*_{k+i\mid k} \in \mathcal{C}_i^{\mathrm{poly}}$ for all $i$.

\noindent \textbf{Verifier Polytope for Expansion.}
The nominal polytope in \eqref{eq:poly_safeset} is a convex inner approximation imposed on all nonzero-weight MPPI rollouts, thereby enabling the convexity-based averaging argument. In a nonconvex free space, however, a single shared region may exclude collision-free rollouts in other local modes, even if that region is enlarged~\cite{deits2015computing}. Therefore, to expand a generative policy into these unexplored safe modes, it is important to preserve the finite-horizon DCBF conditions while reducing the conservatism of the convex inner approximation relative to the nominal polytope. Given a queried action, the verifier instead evaluates its induced rollout individually at the current receding-horizon step, allowing the separating faces to depend on the queried rollout.

For a queried action \(\hat{\mathbf u}\), let
\(\{\xki\}_{i=0}^{H}\) denotes the resulting trajectory. For each convex configuration-space obstacle \(j\in\mathcal O(\xk)\), represented as \(\mathcal O_j:=\{\q\in\mathbb R^d:\|\q-\oj\|_2\le r_j\},\)
the verifier constructs a separating hyperplane \(\mathbf a_j^\top(\mathbf q-\qk)\le m_j,\)
by solving the maximization problem
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\max_{\aj, m_j}\ & m_j\\
\text{s.t. }\ & \aj^\top(\qki-\qk)\le\big(1-(1-\gamma)^i\big)m_j \; (i=1,\dots,H),\\
& r_j\|\aj\|_2\le \aj^\top(\oj-\qk)-m_j,\\
&\|\aj\|_2\le1,\ 0< m_{\min} \le m_j.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:socp}
\end{equation}
Problem~\eqref{eq:socp} is a second-order cone program (SOCP), which we solve using Clarabel through CVXPY~\cite{goulart2026clarabel}. Specifically, the first constraint imposes the safety specification in \eqref{eq:poly} with \(h(\xki;\xk)=1-\aj^\top(\qki-\qk)/m_j\) equal to 1 at the current position and is required to remain at least $(1-\gamma)^i$. The obstacle-separation constraint follows from
\[
\inf_{\q\in\mathcal O_j}\aj^\top(\q-\qk)
=
\aj^\top(\oj-\qk)-r_j\|\aj\|_2
\ge m_j.
\]
The remaining constraints fix the scale of the normal and impose the strictly positive margin $m_{\min}\!>\!0$. 
Let $(\aj^\star,m_j^\star)$ denote a solution of
\eqref{eq:socp} for obstacle $j$. The resulting verifier polytope \footnote{In implementation, we additionally include auxiliary bounding faces around the sensing region, making $\mathcal P$ bounded (e.g., 12 auxiliary faces defined by orange control points are used in Fig.~\ref{fig:validity-verifier-pedestrian}(left)).} for a queried action is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P(\hat{\mathbf u};\o,\gamma):=\nspace{-10}\bigcap_{j\in\mathcal O(\xk)}\nspace{-10}\{
\q\in\mathbb R^d:
{\aj^\star}^{\!\top}(\q-\qk)\le m_j^\star\}.
\label{eq:verifierpolytope}
\end{equation}

For brevity, we abbreviate the \(H\) steps of predicted control sequence (or action) as $\{ \hat{\u}_{k+i\mid k}\}_{i=0}^{H-1}$ as \(\hat{\mathbf u}\). Having formulated the optimization problem to verify the existence of a multi-step safety specification, we formally define the \textit{validity} of a queried control policy.
\begin{definition}
\label{def:verifier}
Let \(v_\gamma(\hat{\mathbf u};\o) \in \{0, 1\}\) be a indicator function that equals $1$ if and only if~\eqref{eq:socp} is feasible for \emph{every} $j\in\mathcal O(\xk)$ at action $\hat{\mathbf u}$, context $\o$, and safety parameter $\gamma$.
The \textit{valid action set} is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)
    :=
    \left\{
    \hat{\mathbf u}\in\mathcal U^H:
    v_\gamma(\hat{\mathbf u};\o)=1
    \right\},
    \label{eq:verifier-certified-set}
\end{equation}
and action \(\hat{\mathbf u} \in \Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)\) is a \textit{valid} action that guarantees the existence of a verifier polytope~\eqref{eq:verifierpolytope}.
\end{definition}

Under the condition below, every action satisfying the nominal-polytope constraints~\ref{eq:nominalpolytope} is a valid action.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:nominal-verifier}
Fix $(\o,\gamma)$ and suppose the configuration-space
obstacles are represented as above. Let $\p_j$ and the
outward unit normal $\n_j$ define the nominal face, and let \(d_j:=\n_j^\top(\qk-\p_j)=\|\qk-\oj\|_2-r_j>0.\)
If $m_{\min}\le\min_{j\in\mathcal O(\xk)}d_j$, then, for every
$\hat{\mathbf u}\in\mathcal U^H$ whose resulting trajectory satisfies
\[
h^{\mathrm{poly}}(\xki;\xk)\ge(1-\gamma)^i,
\quad i=1,\ldots,H,
\]
the choice $(\aj,m_j)=(-\n_j,d_j)$ is feasible
for~\eqref{eq:socp}, and hence
$\hat{\mathbf u}\in\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$.
\end{proposition}
This implication holds for MPPI-DCBF under Assumption~\ref{assumption_1} and the LTI conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:2}. For a general control-affine system, each queried action must be verified separately. 
Furthermore, we can formalize how $\gamma$ explicitly modulates the valid action set. In the following proposition, we show the effect of $\gamma$ during the verification process.
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:gamma-effect}
For any $\o$ and
$0<\gamma_1\le\gamma_2\le1$,
\setlength{\abovedisplayskip}{2pt}
\setlength{\abovedisplayshortskip}{2pt}
\begin{equation}
\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma_1)
\subseteq
\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma_2).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
For every horizon index $i$,
$1-(1-\gamma)^i$ is nondecreasing in $\gamma$.
Thus, any valid action at $\gamma_1$ also satisfies the trajectory constraints at $\gamma_2$, while all remaining constraints in~\eqref{eq:socp} are unchanged. \hfill$\blacksquare$

In offline training, generative policies constrain actions to the distribution of their demonstrations. While Proposition~\ref{prop:nominal-verifier} formally guarantees that the MPPI-DCBF planner allows us to produce rapid valid action priors, relying solely on these demonstrations inherently yields a subset of the distribution of all possible safe actions at given context and required safety level (\textit{e.g.,} $\gamma$). This fundamental limitation strongly motivates us to expand the generative policy distribution beyond the initial demonstrations to discover a broader range of valid actions.

\subsection{Safe Flow Expansion}
We introduce Safe Flow Expansion (Alg.~\ref{alg:sfe}) for the CFM model, which acquires missing safe behavioral modes by first pretraining on the safe action support provided by safety-critical SBPC (\textit{i.e.,} MPPI-DCBF) and iteratively increasing the probability mass on uncertain multi-step safe actions. 

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Safe Flow Expansion}
\label{alg:sfe}
\small
\LinesNumbered
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{pretrained CFM $\theta_0$; verifier $v_{\gamma_k}$; dynamics $f$;
execution cost $C$; rounds $N$; success target $P$;
proposal and verification budgets $K\ge B$}
\KwOut{expanded CFM $\theta_N$}
$\mathcal B_0\gets\varnothing$\;
\For{$n\gets0$ \KwTo $N-1$}{
$\sigma_n\gets\textsc{UpdateUncertainty}(\mathcal B_n)$;\quad
$\mathcal D_n,\mathcal S_n\gets\varnothing$\;
\Repeat{$|\mathcal S_n|\ge P$}{
roll out $\theta_n$ in parallel\;
\ForEach{replanning step $k$ with context $(\o_k,\gamma_k)$}{
draw $\hat{\mathbf u}^{(1:B)}$ from $K$ proposals
by~\eqref{eq:importancesampling}\;
$y_j\gets v_{\gamma_k}(\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j)};\o_k)$,
$\;\mathcal J_k^+\gets\{j:y_j=1\}$\;
$\mathcal D_n\gets\mathcal D_n\cup
\{(\o_k,\gamma_k,\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j)},y_j)\}_{j=1}^{B}$\;
\lIf{$\mathcal J_k^+=\varnothing$}{\textsc{RestartRollout}()}
$j^\star\gets\arg\min_{j\in\mathcal J_k^+}
C(\o_k,\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j)})$\;
$\mathbf u_k\gets[\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j^\star)}]_0$,
$\;\mathbf x_{k+1}\gets f(\mathbf x_k,\mathbf u_k)$\;
}
add every restart-free rollout to $\mathcal S_n$\;
}
$\mathcal B_{n+1}\gets\textsc{UpdateBuffer}(\mathcal B_n,\mathcal D_n)$;\quad

$\theta_{n+1}\gets\textsc{UpdateCFM}(\theta_n,\mathcal D_n^{+})$\;
}
\Return{$\theta_N$}\;
% \vskip -0.1 true in
\end{algorithm}

Concretely, for a fixed \((\o,\gamma)\), the policy at expansion round \(n\) induces the \textit{conditional generable set} associated with the probability density lower bound \(\tau\) defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_{\theta_n}^{\tau}(\o,\gamma)
    :=\{
    \hat{\mathbf u}\in\mathcal U^H:
    p_1^{\theta_n}(\hat{\mathbf u}\mid\o,\gamma)\ge\tau
    \},
    \label{eq:conditional-generable-set}
\end{equation}
where $p_1^{\theta_n}(\mathbf u\mid\o,\gamma)$ denotes the conditional policy density at round $n$.

The conditional generable set contains actions to which the policy assigns density at least $\tau$. Here, we recover the pre-trained conditional generable set by setting \(n=0\).

To self-generate samples informative about multi-step safety while staying near the current policy \(\theta_n\), at expansion round \(n\),
we sample\footnote{This is the exact Gibbs solution of the KL-regularized exploration objective.
\(
p_n \in \arg\max_q \{ \mathbb{E}_q[\sigma_n(\phi_s(\hat\u))] - \beta \text{KL}(q \parallel p_1^{\theta_n})\},
\) where $\text{KL}$ denotes Kulback-Liebler divergence.}
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf u}
\sim
p_1^{\theta_n}(\hat{\mathbf u}\mid\o,\gamma)
\exp\!\left(
\sigma_n\!\left(\phi_s(\hat{\mathbf u};\o,\gamma)\right)/\beta
\right)
/Z_n(\o,\gamma)
,
\label{eq:importancesampling}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
Z_n(\o,\gamma)\!=\!\!\!\int_{\mathcal U^H}\nspace{-15}
p_1^{\theta_n}(\mathbf u\!\mid\!\o,\!\gamma)\exp\!\left(
\sigma_n\!\left(\phi_s(\mathbf u;\o,\!\gamma)\right)/\beta
\right)
d\mathbf u,
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_n$ is the kernel posterior uncertainty returned by \textsc{UpdateUncertainty} in Alg.~\ref{alg:sfe} and $\phi_s$ denotes the penultimate feature of the conditional flow vector field evaluated at the fixed flow-noise level $s$.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{images/demo_3d/beta.pdf}
    \vspace{-6.0mm}
    \caption{Smaller $\beta$ puts more weight on uncertain actions and finds more valid modes in the 3D single-sphere task after the same number of expansion rounds. Colors show the four modes: above, below, left, and right.}
    \label{fig:beta}
    \vspace{-\baselineskip}
\end{figure}

At each replanning step $k$, SFE self-generates a finite batch of actions from the current CFM policy using the verifier uncertainty~\eqref{eq:importancesampling}. The verifier $v_{\gamma_k}$ is defined in Def.~\ref{def:verifier} and separates these queried actions \(\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j)}\) into valid (\textit{i.e., \(j\in\mathcal J^+_k\)}) and invalid sets. The execution cost \(C\) ranks valid action candidates, from which the first action with minimum cost is executed \footnote{With the shared execution cost $\gamma$ affects the action through the admissible set $\mathcal J_k^+$ over which the importance sampling is performed.}, while one can set this cost as expert actions \cite{ross2011reduction} or restrict execution to a safe admissible action set \cite{alshiekh2018safe}. At each expansion round, we repeat this data acquisition until we gather $P$ successful trajectories, and add both valid and invalid sets to the buffer. We add the executed actions to update the model by selecting them uniformly over the $\gamma$ and successful trajectories before the CFM update. Finally, CFM loss~\eqref{eq:cfm-loss} is regressed using the valid sets. 

\textsc{UpdateBuffer} and the subsequent \textsc{UpdateUncertainty} is the key to close the acquisition loop. By updating the kernel posterior uncertainty $\sigma_{n+1}$ with the features $\phi_s$ of both valid sets and invalid sets, the next round verification budget $K$ is naturally tilted toward poorly covered modes. 
A possible concern is that, because the buffer \(\mathcal B_n\) pools observations from finite contexts at each round, \(\sigma_n(\phi_s(\hat{\mathbf u}))\) may prioritize an action due to novelty in unrelated contexts. However, maintaining per-context uncertainty is impractical, since no finite buffer can cover the space of planning contexts--the same issues that have arisen that make conditional generative planners struggle to generalize to OOD environments without retraining \cite{ye2024efficient, dai2025safefm}. We therefore use the buffer only as an acquisition heuristic to actively explore unvisited modes \cite{de2026active}: every selected candidate is re-evaluated by \(v_\gamma(\hat{\mathbf u};\o)\) under the current context, so buffering finite samples can affect the query but not validity.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol and Implementation}
\noindent\textbf{Evaluation protocol.}
% \noindent \textbf{Train and test data details}
% The specific training and test configurations are as follows:
To generate a dataset for policy learning, we use the proposed MPPI-DCBF planner with 2D and 3D double-integrator dynamics and a sampling time of $\Delta t = 0.1s$. We generated all data and trained all models on a workstation equipped with an NVIDIA H100 NVL GPU, and we tested online trajectory generation on an NVIDIA RTX 4060 Ti GPU. Across four motion-planning tasks, we structure the evaluation into three phases: initial in-distribution training, SFE in OOD environments, and final OOD testing, while keeping the start, goal, and robot dynamics fixed. After pretraining, SFE (Alg.~\ref{alg:sfe}) collects verified actions in the OOD environments. We evaluate SFE under two OOD protocols. In \emph{configuration-specific adaptation}, expansion and evaluation use the same OOD obstacle configuration; this protocol includes the 2D cluttered environment and the 3D single-sphere environment. In \emph{cross-configuration OOD generalization}, evaluation uses independently sampled OOD configurations that are not used during expansion; this protocol includes the 2D dynamic-pedestrian environment and the 3D cluttered environment. After each expansion round $n$, we evaluate the expanded model $\theta_n$ independently with 16 function evaluations (NFE) across multiple values of $\gamma$.

For the default validity and mode coverage evaluations, SFE generates a single ($J\!=\!1$) control sequence at each replanning step, where the computational and behavioral effects of larger $J$ are reported in Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}(b) and Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff}, respectively. To account for the varying difficulty of the four tasks, we use a task-specific success target $P$ and hold it fixed across the ten expansion rounds.

\noindent \textbf{Task configurations:}
In the \emph{2D cluttered environment}, The robot starts at \((0.3,0.3)\) and navigates toward the fixed goal at \((4.7,4.7)\) in a \(5\,\mathrm{m}\times5\,\mathrm{m}\) taskspace. The CFM model is pretrained on an MPPI-DCBF policy from a random initial position to a fixed goal position, total 200k actions of length $H$ as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:2d_cluttered}, in a nominal environment with regularly sized circular obstacles. We tested this by replacing the central obstacles with a previously unseen large obstacle to see whether SFE can learn long-horizon, collision-free behavior and avoid getting trapped in local minima.
        
In the \emph{2D dynamic-pedestrian environment}, the robot starts at \((0,0)\) and moves to \((6,6)\), with the simulated pedestrian environment modeled by the social force model~\cite{helbing1995social}, where each pedestrian is a modeled reactive dynamical system whose acceleration exponentially grows as agents approach. In these challenging dynamic tasks, the policy conditions on the 10-step history of local observations $h^\text{poly}$, unlike other tasks. We tested this to capture how SFE can learn highly reactive behaviors under dynamic environments.

For the \emph{3D hardware environments}, we deploy online 3D motion planning on a \textit{Crazyflie} quadrotor. The robot starts at \((-2.5,1.5,0.9)\) to \((0.7,-1.5,0.9)\) in a \(4\,\mathrm{m}\times4\,\mathrm{m}\times1.5\,\mathrm{m}\) taskspace. For both single-sphere and cluttered environments, the CFM model is pretrained in environments with $4-8$ randomly positioned cylinder obstacles within the taskspace range. In the former configuration, a sphere is placed at the midpoint between the start and the goal; in the latter, it uses $6$ independent randomized spheres. For the mode coverage test (Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}), we fixed the test configuration with $6$ spheres to compare the pretrained model, the expanded model with $5$ rounds, full rounds of $10$, and the baseline method.

The policy replans at $10$ Hz using the measured position and a finite-difference velocity estimate. The resulting acceleration command is streamed at $100$ Hz as the feedforward component of a full-state setpoint, whose position and velocity components are obtained by one-step integration of the double-integrator model.

\noindent \textbf{Baseline method} We compared against CFM-MPPI~\cite{mizuta2025unified}, which combines reward-guided flow generation with MPPI refinement. Given a base pre-trained vector field $\mathbf{v}^{\theta_{\text{0}}}(\mathbf{x}\!\mid\! \mathbf{o},\gamma)$, the guided velocity field is the local gradient ascent of the combined reward:
\begin{equation}
   \tilde{\mathbf{v}}^{\theta_{\text{0}}}(\mathbf{x}_\tau\!\mid\! \mathbf{o},\gamma)  = \mathbf{v}^{\theta_{\text{0}}}(\mathbf{x}_\tau\!\mid\! \mathbf{o},\gamma) \;+\; \eta \nabla_{\mathbf{z}_1}R(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z}_1),
   \label{eq:cfm-mppi_vectorfield}
\end{equation}
The guided policy locally increases the reward that is designed to encourage both performance and safety
\begin{equation}
    R(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\hat u}) = \sum_{k=0}^{H-1} w_\text{s} r_\text{s}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\hat u}) + w_\text{g} r_\text{g} (\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\hat u}), 
    \label{eq:cfm-mppi_guidance}
\end{equation}
where $w_\text{s}, w_\text{g}$ are scalar weights. The safety reward term $r_\text{s}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\hat u})$ is defined to penalize violations of the CBF condition~\cite[Eq. 6]{mizuta2025unified}.

We implement the mode-selective variant, the strongest configuration they report: at each receding-horizon step, CFM-MPPI generates $J=$32 guided control sequences from the same pretrained model used for expansion. The $8$ lowest-cost priors were perturbed $M=$32 times with Gaussian noise,
%, giving $256$ candidates
and averaging with MPPI weights produces $8$ refined control sequences from which the minimum-cost action is executed. The method then warm-starts the next replan at flow time $t=0.75$. For each task, we evaluated various combinations of the scalar weights and selected the pair that provided the best trade-off between safety (collision rate) and task performance (time-to-goal).

\noindent\textbf{Configuration-specific adaptation.}
Fig.~\ref{fig:2d_cluttered} isolates the 2D adaptation setting. We replaced central obstacles with one large obstacle that blocks the short paths represented in the demonstrations, so reaching the goal requires long-horizon planning. At both tested values of $w_{\mathrm{s}}$, the baseline method does not reliably recover this behavior, whereas SFE learns collision-free paths around the obstacle. The resulting closed-loop behavior indicates that expansion has shifted the policy distribution toward verified, goal-reaching actions rather than relying on a larger deployment-time candidate set.

The 3D single-sphere task provides a controlled adaptation setting for the uncertainty-weighted sampling~\eqref{eq:importancesampling}. Fig.~\ref{fig:beta} compares the expansion setting $\beta=0.1$ with lower uncertainty weighting at $\beta=1.0$ and standard sampling at $\beta\to\infty$, while holding the remaining SFE training loop and the number of expansion rounds fixed. Smaller $\beta$ places more weight on uncertain actions and recovers more of the above, below, left, and right modes.

\noindent\textbf{Cross-configuration OOD generalization.}
For the dynamic pedestrian environments, we use $(K,B)=(64,32)$ to efficiently acquire valid actions. The verifier uses the currently observed obstacle geometry over the prediction horizon and therefore certifies finite-horizon validity only with respect to that frozen scene. We use a constant-velocity model of the pedestrians only in the execution cost to favor valid action candidates that anticipate future collisions; collision avoidance against moving pedestrians otherwise relies empirically on receding-horizon replanning.

For 3D hardware experiments, the MPPI-DCBF demonstrations are collected among vertical cylinders and are concentrated on planar lateral modes. The same bias is illustrated for the single-sphere setting in Fig.~\ref{fig:hero}(C). In the unseen cluttered environment, the pretrained CFM consequently remains close to the demonstration altitude, as shown by the two left columns of Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}(a). 
However, as SFE iteratively updates valid OOD actions back into training, the policy discovers novel lateral, over, and under modes absent from the pretraining data across the expansion rounds (second to fourth columns in Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}(a)). The baseline method frequently collides, where the local guidance leads the robot toward the free space (\textit{e.g.,} over and under the balls) but the pretrained base flow $\mathbf{v}^{\theta_{\text{0}}}$ in~\eqref{eq:cfm-mppi_vectorfield} has a generable set that lacks enough probability mass to recover safety. In contrast, SFE shifts probability mass toward fundamentally new safe modes trained offline.

\subsection{Offline expansion computation time}
The 10-round offline expansion required approximately $1$ GPU hour on a single NVIDIA H100 NVL GPU in \emph{3D cluttered environments} for Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}. In the profiled second expansion round, \textsc{UpdateUncertainty} (line~3), which refits the GP once at the beginning of each round, took negligible time $<0.1\%$. The acquisition loop (lines~4--14) accounted for $72\%$, where CFM proposal generation (line~7) used $11\%$, uncertainty evaluation and acquisition in(line~7) used \emph{$27\%$}, verifier evaluation (line~8) used \emph{$24\%$}, and rollout $10\%$. After the loop, \textsc{UpdateBuffer} (line~15) accounted for $7\%$, \textsc{UpdateCFM} regression (line~16) accounted for $21\%$. Thus, offline computation cost is dominated by uncertainty-guided self-generation~\eqref{eq:importancesampling} and the verification process~\eqref{eq:socp}.

\subsection{Safe Flow Expansion Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

This section connects the MPPI-DCBF demonstrations to the initial set for SFE and a conditional lower bound on valid-action availability (Details on the fixed-context analysis model and generable set coverage are deferred to~\Cref{app:sfe-analysis}). All results apply separately to each fixed $(\o,\gamma)$. Extending the analysis across the full observation and safety parameter space remains future work.

For a validity threshold $p_{\min}\in(0,1)$ and $\epsilon\in(0,1-p_{\min})$, we denote a measurable $S_0\subseteq\mathcal U^H$ an \textit{initial safe set} if $\operatorname{Vol}(S_0)$ is well defined and positive, $S_0\subseteq\Omega^{\tau}_{\theta_0}(\o,\gamma)\cap\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$, and $\inf_{\hat{\mathbf u}\in S_0}\varsigma(g(\phi_s(\hat{\mathbf u};\o,\gamma))) \ge p_{\min}+\epsilon$. Let $R_\epsilon$ denote the induced action-space one-step reachability operator of~\cite[Eq.~(19)]{de2026active}, with $\mathcal X=\mathcal U^H$ and $\phi=\phi_s(\cdot;\o,\gamma)$. We use  $R_\epsilon^r$ for $r$ repeated applications of this operator.

The MPPI-DCBF demonstrations provide only finitely many verified actions, which by themselves have zero volume in the action space and therefore cannot form the initial set required by the expansion analysis. The following lemma bridges this gap, where strict feasibility and continuity enlarge each verified action into a small safe neighborhood, whose union provides a nonzero-volume initial safe set for SFE.

\begin{lemma}[Initial safe set from MPPI-DCBF]
\label{lem:seed}
Under the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:nominal-verifier}, let $Q\ge1$ and let $\{\hat{\mathbf u}_0^{(q)}\}_{q=1}^{Q}\subset\operatorname{int}(\mathcal U^H)$
be a finite set of MPPI-DCBF actions satisfying~\eqref{eq:nominalpolytope}
strictly, with $p_1^{\theta_0}(\hat{\mathbf u}_0^{(q)}\mid\o,\gamma)>\tau$ and
$\varsigma(g(\phi_s(\hat{\mathbf u}_0^{(q)};\o,\gamma)))>p_{\min}+\epsilon$.
If the dynamics used by the planner in~\eqref{eq:affinesystem} are continuous in the state and action,
% If the resulting finite-horizon trajectory depends continuously on $\hat{\mathbf u}$ at these actions, 
and
$p_1^{\theta_0}(\cdot\mid\o,\gamma)$ and $\varsigma\circ g\circ\phi_s$ are
continuous, then there exist radii $\rho_q>0$ such that
$S_0:=\bigcup_q B_{\rho_q}(\hat{\mathbf u}_0^{(q)})$ is an initial seed set \footnote{Here, \(B_\rho(\bar{\mathbf u})\) denotes the open Euclidean ball \(\left\{\hat{\mathbf u}\!\in\!\mathcal U^H\!:\!\|\hat{\mathbf u}\!-\!\bar{\mathbf u}\|_2\!<\!\rho\right\}\).}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By continuity of the dynamics in~\eqref{eq:affinesystem}, the induced
finite-horizon trajectory map is continuous in $\hat{\mathbf u}$. 
Since all relevant conditions are strict, they remain true for actions sufficiently close to each $\hat{\mathbf u}_0^{(q)}$. Choose each $\rho_q$ small enough that its ball remains inside $\mathcal U^H$ and preserves these conditions. \Cref{prop:nominal-verifier} then places every such ball in $\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:seed} does not require the MPPI-DCBF demonstrations to cover all $\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$. It only requires a finite set of actions that satisfies the strict conditions and remains valid under sufficiently small action changes.

Starting from this initial safe set, Lemma~\ref{lem:coverage} guarantees that after $T^\star=rT$ sequential query and model-update steps in Appendix~\ref{app:sfe-analysis} at a fixed $(\o,\gamma)$, the coverage event $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{cov}}\!:=\!\{R_\epsilon^r(S_0)\!\subseteq\!\Omega_{\theta_{T^\star}}^\tau\!(\o,\gamma)\}$ holds with probability at least $1-\delta$, \textit{i.e.,} $\Pr(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{cov}})\ge1-\delta$. This gives the following sampling guarantee.

\begin{corollary}[Probability of valid action after expansion]
\label{thm:safe-action-availability}
Under the setting above, let $r\!\ge\!1$ be an integer and define
$\mathcal A_{\gamma,r}(\o)\!:=\!R_\epsilon^r(S_0)\cap\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$
and $m_{\gamma,r}(\o):=\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal A_{\gamma,r}(\o))$. Then $m_{\gamma,r}(\o)\ge\operatorname{Vol}(S_0)>0$, and $m_{\gamma,r}(\o)$ is nondecreasing in $r$. 
Conditioned on the expansion history, independently sample $J\ge1$ actions from $p_1^{\theta_{T^\star}}(\cdot\mid\o,\gamma)$. Accounting for both the expansion process and the $J$ samples, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\Pr\!\left[\exists\,\!j\!\le\! J\!:\! v_\gamma(\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j)};\!\o)\!=\!1\right]
% &\!\ge\!
% (1-\delta)\!\left[1-\!\bigl(1\!-\!\tau m_{\gamma,r}(\o)\!\bigr)^{J}\right]\!\\
\!\ge\!
(1-\delta)\!\left[1\!-\!e^{\!-J\tau m_{\gamma,r}(\o)}\right]\!\!.
\label{eq:main1}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By definition, the reachability operator contains its input and preserves set inclusion. Therefore, it holds that $S_0\subseteq\!R_\epsilon^r(S_0)\subseteq\! R_\epsilon^{r+1}(S_0)$. Intersecting with $\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)\supseteq S_0$ gives the volume bound. On the coverage event $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{cov}}$, every action in $\mathcal A_{\gamma,r}(\o)$ is a valid action and has density at least $\tau$. Independence of the $J$ samples gives the conditional bound \(1-\left(1-\tau m_{\gamma,r}(\o)\right)^J\), using \(1\!-\!x\!\le \!e^{-x}\), together with $\Pr(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{cov}})\ge1-\delta$ gives~\eqref{eq:main1}.
\end{proof}

The lower bound in~\eqref{eq:main1} is nondecreasing with $r$ as $m_{\gamma,r}(\o)$ is nondecreasing by the monotonicity of the reachability operator as described in Lemma~\ref{lem:coverage}, for fixed $(\o,\gamma,S_0,\epsilon,\tau,\delta,J)$~\footnote{This conclusion neither assumes that the conditional generable set for one $r$ is contained in the next nor uses the same coverage condition for different $r$. The claim applies only to the guaranteed lower bound, and it does not claim that the validity of each later CFM policy must increase.}. Also, not only the effect of $r$ but also the effect of $\gamma$ can be noted. Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-effect} establishes an inclusion between valid action sets $\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$ at different values of $\gamma$, whereas Corollary~\ref{thm:safe-action-availability} applies separately to each fixed $(\o,\gamma)$~\footnote{Changing $\gamma$ may also change the conditional policy, learned representation, initial safe set, and reachability operator.}. Therefore, Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-effect} by itself does not imply an inclusion between the corresponding reachable or conditional generable sets, and hence does not imply monotonicity of $m_{\gamma,r}(\o)$ or~\eqref{eq:main1} in $\gamma$.

\section{Conclusion}

Our next step is to scale the framework to systems with extremely large state spaces or where expert demonstrations are prohibitively expensive. In parallel, while our method robustly expands the safe action distribution, tuning the online execution cost $C$ can still shift the distribution away from the prior actions, which remains an important challenge.

While under the stated assumptions in~\Cref{sec:analysis}, Corollary~\ref{thm:safe-action-availability} lower-bounds, at a fixed $(\o,\gamma)$, the probability that a generated batch of actions contains a valid action.

Future work could explore combining our expanded policy with a lightweight run-time safety filter to provide hard guarantees.

Additionally, learning an optimal $\gamma$ schedule directly from data remains an interesting open problem (\textit{e.g.,} rather than a fixed $\gamma$ during deployment). Such dynamic scheduling would allow the system to tightly trade off safety and performance, which can be critical in real-world tasks where the robot must adjust caution to context.
