\begin{abstract}
Generative policies pretrained on safe demonstrations often perform poorly in out-of-distribution environments because they inherit the training data's limited behavior support, and inference-time guidance only reweights samples from that fixed distribution rather than expanding what the policy can generate. We introduce \emph{Safe Flow Expansion} (SFE), an offline framework that iteratively self-generates actions from a conditional flow matching (CFM) policy, verifies them against finite-horizon discrete-time control barrier function (DCBF) conditions, and updates the policy to expand its set of valid actions. 
SFE builds on MPPI-DCBF, our proposed sampling-based planner that rejects rollouts leaving a convex nominal polytope before averaging. Under stated conditions, MPPI-DCBF certifies the executed step and provides demonstrations modulated by safety parameter $\gamma$. We derive a conditional lower bound on the probability that a batch of generated actions contains a valid action. In 2D and 3D simulation and on Crazyflie hardware, the expanded policy achieves higher validity and mode coverage than pretrained and guided baselines. We also showed that it replans with far lower latency than online planners, and $\gamma$ tunes the conservativeness of both the planner and the policy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Sampling-based predictive control (SBPC) is gaining traction as an optimal control framework. General optimal control approaches often suffer from the computational complexity that is associated with solving stochastic Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman equations backward in time. SBPC is an alternative to gradient-based model predictive control (MPC), where a simple sampling procedure replaces a sophisticated nonlinear optimizer~\cite{posa2014direct}. 
Algorithms in this family include model predictive path integral control (MPPI)~\cite{williams2016aggressive}, predictive sampling~\cite{howell2022predictive}, Generative Predictive Control~\cite{Kurtz_GPC}, and cross-entropy methods~\cite{rubinstein1999cross}. MPPI, for example, circumvents computational complexity by employing forward sampling of stochastic dynamics combined with Monte Carlo approximation techniques~\cite{williams2016aggressive}. 

SBPC algorithms are particularly well-suited to recent advances in parallel computing architectures~\cite{mjx, makoviychuk2021isaac}, enabling them to scale to complex problems like in-hand dexterous manipulation~\cite{li2025drop}, legged locomotion~\cite{xue2025full}, and more~\cite{howell2022predictive, Kurtz_GPC}.

Despite these advantages, SBPCs such as MPPI, inherently struggle with safety guarantees. Safety constraints are typically incorporated indirectly through penalty terms in the path planning cost function~\cite{williams2017model, yin2023shield}. While this approach encourages safe behavior, it lacks explicit safety guarantees because the procedure relies on sampling and averaging. 

Even if individual sampled trajectories satisfy safety constraints, their weighted average may not. This limitation motivates our first goal of integrating safety-critical constraints explicitly into SBPC frameworks. To this end, we introduce MPPI-DCBFs, which prevent such failure modes by restricting all nonzero-weight rollouts to a common convex polytope before averaging.

A common method to ensure safety in collision-avoidance tasks is to impose finite-horizon control barrier function (CBF) constraints directly within MPC~\cite{zeng2021enhancing}, under nonlinear dynamics and nonconvex obstacle geometry. However, the resulting optimization can be nonconvex and computationally heavy. This computational burden motivates a shift from online constrained optimization to learning-based methods, where complex planning is performed offline to curate datasets of safe and multi-modal behavior. Generative models, such as diffusion and flow matching (FM)~\cite{lipman2022flow}, can synthesize motion trajectories and action sequences from diverse, high-dimensional demonstrations~\cite{chi2023diffusion, Kurtz_GPC}. Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) has emerged as a promising alternative to diffusion-based modeling, offering competitive generation quality with efficient sampling~\cite{chisari2024learning, ye2024efficient}. 
% CFM is particularly useful in safety-critical motion planning and control, as it can generate high-fidelity, multi-modal trajectories with fewer inference time steps. 
% Maintaining this topological diversity is crucial to avoid committing to a single local solution that may become infeasible as the scene changes~\cite{de2024topology, shafiullah2022behavior}. 
However, they do not natively provide formal safety guarantees during rollout. 

Recent CFM-based works have further incorporated explicit safety constraints at inference time by trajectory regularization or constrained generation~\cite{li2026hardflow,utkarsh2026physics}, while MPC-based refinement has been used to improve local optimality of generated trajectories~\cite{mizuta2025unified}. However, these methods guide or refine samples from a fixed pretrained distribution. In out-of-distribution (OOD) contexts, the pretrained model may assign low probability to safe and multi-modal behaviors, and aggressive guidance can further push generation away from its learned distribution~\cite{dandapanthula2026we}.

Consequently, a fundamental challenge in generative motion planning and control is to address this distribution shift by expanding the generable set of a pretrained policy toward safe and multi-modal regions of action. Recent work formalizes this goal as \emph{generable set expansion}~\cite{de2026active}, which expands the range of behaviors the FM model can generate. We extend this concept to a CFM model to discover new safe behaviors in robotics motion planning. To this end, we introduce the \textit{Safe Flow Expansion} framework, which expands the pretrained policy toward new safe behaviors using a safety-critical predictive planner and verifier mechanisms. Under the stated safety conditions, MPPI-DCBF can be deployed as an online receding-horizon controller while also generating certified demonstrations for offline CFM training.

\textbf{Contributions.} Our contributions are threefold:
(i) Safe Flow Expansion, an offline framework that expands a CFM policy's valid generable set by uncertainty-guided self-generation, finite-horizon DCBF verification, and continued pretraining, discovering valid OOD modes the pretrained policy misses.
(ii) MPPI-DCBF, an online planner that rejects sampled rollouts violating finite-horizon DCBF conditions before weighted averaging, preserving the safety certificate of the executed next state under control-affine dynamics and the entire predicted horizon for LTI systems.
(iii) An analysis carrying safety parameter $\gamma$ from planner to verifier to policy, giving, under stated assumptions, a per-context lower bound on the probability that a generated batch contains a valid action. We evaluate our idea in 2D cluttered and pedestrian environments and on a 3D quadrotor, in simulation and on hardware.

\section{Preliminaries}
\subsection{Model Predictive Path Integral control}
In discrete time, at state $\xk\in\mathbb R^{n_x}$, MPPI optimizes a mean control sequence $\{ \uki \}_{i=0}^{H-1}$ in a convex admissible set $\mathcal U$ over horizon $H$ by forward sampling and Monte Carlo reweighting~\cite{williams2016aggressive}, warm started from the previous solution shifted one step, $\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{k+i\mid k}$. For rollout $m=1,\dots,M$ with perturbations $\varepsilon^{(m)}_{k+i}\!\sim\!\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)$, the sampled controls, states, and costs are
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
&\mathbf{u}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}=\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{k+i\mid k}+\varepsilon^{(m)}_{k+i},\label{eq:mppi_rollout}\\
&\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+i+1\mid k}=f\!\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k},\,\mathbf{u}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}\bigr),\\
&S^{(m)}\!=\!\phi\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+H\mid k}\bigr)
\!+\!\!\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}\Big(\!q\,\!\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}\bigr)\!+\!\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{u}^{(m)\top}_{k+i\mid k} R\,\mathbf{u}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k}\Big)\label{eq:mppicost}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
with running cost $q: \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$, terminal cost $\phi : \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$,  control disturbance variance $\Sigma$, control weight $R \in \mathbb{R}^{n_u \times n_u}$. Soft-min weights with temperature $\lambda>0$, stabilized by subtracting $S_\text{min}$, define the update for $i\in\{1,\cdots,H-1\}$,
\begin{equation}
\omega_m\!:=\!\exp(-(S^{(m)}\!\!-\!S_{\min})/{\lambda}),\; S_{\min}\!:=\!\!\!\min_{\ell=1,\ldots,M}S^{(\ell)}.
\label{eq:mppi-weights}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Delta \mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}\!=\!
\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{M}\omega_m\,\varepsilon^{(m)}_{k+i}}{\sum_{m=1}^{M}\omega_m},
\mathbf{u}^\star_{k+i\mid k}\!\leftarrow\!\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{k+i\mid k}\!+\!\Delta \mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}. \label{eq:mppi-update}
\end{equation}
of which only $\uk^\star$ is applied before replanning. This parallelizable sampling process makes MPPI well-suited to solving non-convex optimal control problems on modern GPU hardware. However, a key challenge arises in safety-critical applications. Prior work either biases the sampling distribution toward safety without certifying it~\cite{yin2022,yin2023shield}, or executes the first input of the lowest-cost rollout under a CBF filter~\cite{rabiee2024guaranteed}.

\subsection{Conditional Flow Matching for trajectory generation}
CFM is increasingly used in robotics for trajectory generation and policy generation \cite{Kurtz_GPC,mizuta2025unified}.
Trajectory generation learns a mapping from noise to entire collision-free trajectories via end-to-end supervised learning, but it can struggle to generalize to OOD environments and may require complete retraining \cite{ye2024efficient, dai2025safefm}. We train our CFM policy on MPPI-DCBF expert action sequences. Following the MPPI update in~\eqref{eq:mppi-update} let $\z:=\mathbf u^\star_{k:k+H-1|k}$. The model learns the distribution of $\z$ given the observation $\o$ and $\gamma$ (\textit{i.e.,} safety parameter).

For $\epsilon\!\sim\!\mathcal N(0,I_d)$ and $\z\!\sim\! p_{\mathrm{data}}$, we use an optimal transport conditional probability path $\x_t\!=\!(1-t)\epsilon+t\z$, whose sample-wise velocity is $\dot{\x}_t=\z-\epsilon$~\cite{lipman2022flow}. 
The conditional flow-matching objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta)=
\mathbb{E}_{\substack{
t\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1],\;
\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_d),\\
(\z,\o,\gamma)\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}
}}
\left[
\left\|
\mathbf v_t^\theta(\x_t\mid\o,\gamma)-(\z-\epsilon)
\right\|^2
\right].
\label{eq:cfm-loss}
\end{equation}
At inference, an action sequence (hereafter, an \emph{action}) is generated by integrating 
$\dot{\x}_t=\mathbf v_t^\theta(\x_t\mid\o,\gamma)$
from $\x_0\sim\mathcal N(0,I_d)$ to $t=1$, from which the first action is executed in receding horizon fashion.

\subsection{Generable Set Expansion}
% In offline training, generative policies constrain actions to the distribution of their demonstrations. 
Recent work measures effective coverage by the \textit{$\tau$-generable set} $\Omega^\tau_\theta\!:=\!\{\x:p_1^\theta(\x)\ge\tau\}$, the region a FM model samples with non-negligible probability under a finite budget~\cite{de2026active}. Each expansion round self-generates candidates, labels them with a black-box verifier, and continues pretraining on the accepted samples. Candidates are acquired where the verifier is most uncertain, under a KL-regularized tilt of strength $\beta$ toward the current model. With calibrated verifier feedback~\cite{pasztor2024bandits}, finitely many verified queries then suffice, with high probability, for the generable set to contain samples that are reachable in boundedly many local steps~\cite{de2026active}. We adopt this view for a $\gamma$-conditioned CFM policy with a finite-horizon DCBF verifier in place of the black-box oracle, and Section~\ref{sec:analysis} provides the theoretical analysis. 


\subsection{Discrete-Time Control Barrier Functions}
Let $\mathcal{C}=\{\x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_x}:\; h(\x)\ge 0\}$ denote the \emph{safe set} for a continuously differentiable $h$.
For the discrete‐time control‐affine system
\begin{equation}
    \x_{k+1}=f(\x_k)+g(\x_k)\u_k, \label{eq:affinesystem}
\end{equation}
A function $h$ is a discrete‐time control barrier function (DCBF) associated with the control system \eqref{eq:affinesystem} if there exists $\gamma\in(0,1]$ such that for all $\x_k$ there exists a control $\u_k$ with
\begin{equation}
    \Delta h(\x_k,\u_k)\;:=\;h(\x_{k+1})-h(\x_k)\;\ge\;-\gamma\,h(\x_k).
    \label{eq:dtcbf}
\end{equation}
If \eqref{eq:dtcbf} holds at every step, then \(h(\x_k)\!\ge\!(1-\gamma)^{k}\,h(\x_0)\), so the fixed set $\mathcal{C}$ is forward invariant~\cite{ames2014cbf,agrawal2017discrete}.

Let \(\piq:\mathbb R^{n_x} \to \mathbb R^d\) be the linear projection that maps states into the robot positions, \(\piq(\xki)=\qki, i=1,\cdots,H\). For a convex configuration-space obstacle $j$, with closest boundary point $\p_j$ to the current position $\qk$and outward normal $\n_j$, the supporting hyperplane there gives the affine barrier function
\setlength{\belowdisplayskip}{3pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayshortskip}{3pt}
\begin{equation}
h_j^{\mathrm{aff}}(\x;\xk) :=
\frac{\n_j^\top(\q-\p_j)}{\n_j^\top(\q_{k\mid k}-\mathbf{p_j})}.
\label{eq:aff_barrier}
\end{equation}
Since $\n_j^\top(\qk-\p_j)>0$, the safe set for each horizon $i$ can be defined as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{aff}}_{i,j}=\{\x \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \mid h_j^{\mathrm{aff}}(\x;\xk)\ge (1-\gamma)^i\},\label{eq:aff_safeset}
\end{equation}
is a convex half-space. Iterating the condition \eqref{eq:dtcbf} from $i=0$ with the normalization $h_j^{\mathrm{aff}}(\xk;\xk)=1$ yields
\begin{equation}
h_j^{\mathrm{aff}}(\xki;\xk) \ge (1-\gamma)^i. \quad i = 1, \ldots, H.
\label{eq:safetyspecification}
\end{equation}
It applies to any convex obstacle through its closest boundary point and outward normal, and to non-convex ones through a convex hull or decomposition.

\noindent \textbf{Nominal Polytope for MPPI safety.} A \textit{nominal polytope} generalize this to multiple obstacles. Let $\mathcal O(\xk)$ index the obstacles observed at $\xk$. We define the ego-centric (\textit{i.e.}, defined as a function of $\xk$) safety function
\begin{equation}
    h^{\mathrm{poly}}(\x;\xk) = \min_{j\in\mathcal O(\xk)} h^{\mathrm{aff}}_j(\x;\x_{k\mid k})
\end{equation}
Accordingly, the safe set for each horizon,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{poly}}_i=\{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \mid h^{\mathrm{poly}}(\x;\xk)\ge (1-\gamma)^i\},\label{eq:poly_safeset}
\end{equation}
We define this safe set $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{poly}}_i$ as the nominal polytope, and it is a convex inner approximation set of the nonconvex free space, an intersection of half-spaces separating the robot from obstacles. 
% Furthermore, these sets are nested, \(\mathcal C_1^{\mathrm{poly}}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq \mathcal C_H^{\mathrm{poly}}\), and rebuilt at each replanning step.
Because $(1-\gamma)^i\geq 0$, any $\x\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{poly}}_i$ satisfies
$h_j^{\mathrm{aff}}(\x;\xk)\geq 0$ for every $j\in\mathcal O(\xk)$.
For each obstacle, this inequality places $\q$ in the obstacle-free half-space
bounded by the supporting hyperplane at $\p_j$ 
% We claim neither forward invariance of these time-varying sets nor recursive feasibility, relying on the condition below. 
Assumption~\ref{assumption_1} ensures that the set of certified trajectories is non-empty at each replanning state.
% Furthermore, it is constructed as a bounded set by incorporating bounding faces within a sensing range, which will be discussed further.
% While the proper design of terminal ingredients (\textit{i.e.,} a terminal set and cost) can formally guarantee recursive feasibility in traditional MPC, the non-convex nature of the resulting optimization problem often makes real-time implementation computationally intractable \cite{rawlings2020model}. SBPC methods such as MPPI face similar challenges. Rather than proving recursive feasibility, our approach ensures safety by enforcing DCBF constraints at each time step, which relies on the weaker but more practical condition of pointwise feasibility, formalized in the following assumption.

\begin{assumption}[Pointwise feasibility]
\label{assumption_1}
For the discrete-time control affine system \eqref{eq:affinesystem}, for every replanning state $\xk$, and safety parameter $\gamma \in (0,1]$, 
there exists a control sequence\footnote{Assumption~\ref{assumption_1} is the condition for pointwise feasibility of the MPPI-DCBF algorithm at the current state. It ensures that the set of DCBF-certified trajectories for the planner to find is non-empty.}
$\{ \uki \}_{i=0}^{H-1}\in\mathcal U^H$ such that the resulting trajectory $\{\xki\}_{i=1}^{H}$ lies within the sequence of safe sets defined in~\eqref{eq:poly_safeset}, i.e., $\xki \in \mathcal{C}_i^{\mathrm{poly}}$, thus following condition is satisfied:
\begin{equation}
h^{\mathrm{poly}}(\xki;\xk)\ge(1-\gamma)^i,
\quad i=1,\ldots,H.\label{eq:feasibility}
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\section{Problem Definition}
\label{sec:problemdefinition}
We pose safe planning as a constrained optimization problem. For any ego-centric safety function, $h(\cdot;\xk)$, whose nonnegative superlevel set lies in free space, frozen over the horizon and, without loss of generality, normalized by $h(\xk;\xk)=1$: 
%The formulation is \textit{agnostic} to that choice:

\setlength{\abovedisplayskip}{2pt}
\setlength{\abovedisplayshortskip}{2pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayskip}{2pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayshortskip}{2pt}
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:problemdef}
\begin{alignat}{2}
& &&\min_{\mathbf{u}_{k:k+H-1\mid k}}\quad
 \mathbb{E}\!\left[
J(\mathbf{x}_{k:k+H\mid k},
  \mathbf{u}_{k:k+H-1\mid k})
\right]\\
& \text{s.t.}\;
&& \mathbf{x}_{k+i+1\mid k}\!=\!f(\mathbf{x}_{k+i\mid k},\mathbf{u}_{k+i\mid k}),\; i\!=\!0,\ldots,H-1,\\
&&& \mathbf{x}_{k\mid k}\!=\!\mathbf{x}_k,
\mathbf{x}_{k:k+H\mid k}\!\in\!\mathcal{X}^{H+1},
\mathbf{u}_{k:k+H-1\mid k}\!\in\!\mathcal{U}^{H},\\
&&& h(\mathbf{x}_{k+i\mid k};\mathbf{x}_k) \!\ge\! (1-\gamma)^i,\; i\!=\!1,\ldots,H.
\label{eq:poly}
\end{alignat}
\end{subequations}

Direct optimization with a non-convex $h$ does not generally guarantee recursive feasibility for this problem and often makes real-time implementation computationally intractable~\cite{rawlings2020model}. 
We therefore keep a convex inner approximation of the free space and impose, in a receding-horizon fashion, that the evolution of $h$ follows the decay $(1-\gamma)^i$ with respect to the horizon $i$ rather than a fixed level.
From the view of set invariance, $\gamma$ modulates the trade-off between \textit{safety and feasibility}~\cite{zeng2021enhancing}. For example, a small $\gamma$ restricts the safety function to higher values, at the cost of reduced feasibility.

To be compatible with the averaging in MPPI, we assumed that all rollouts \eqref{eq:mppi_rollout} assigned nonzero MPPI weight after rejection based on \eqref{eq:dtcbf}. In practice, we trained the generative policy only on feasible action sets.

We propose MPPI with DCBF-based sample rejection (MPPI-DCBF), which enforces the safety specification~\eqref{eq:poly} with $h=h^\text{poly}$ inside the MPPI sampling step, which is designed to guarantee the forward invariance of the sequence of time-varying safe sets $\mathcal{C}_i^{\mathrm{poly}}$ defined in \eqref{eq:poly_safeset}. This is achieved by integrating the DCBF condition directly into the MPPI sampling step. It rejects every rollout that violates the condition, followed by the MPPI averaging process~\eqref{eq:mppi-update} as a convex combination of only rollouts certified to be safe~\footnote{In practice, if the planner accepts no rollouts, it reports infeasibility.}
%or whose controls leave $\mathcal U$
Under the pointwise feasibility assumption (Assumption.~\ref{assumption_1}), we can formally analyze the properties of the MPPI-DCBF averaging process.

\begin{proposition} \label{prop:1}
    For the discrete-time control affine system \eqref{eq:affinesystem}, if all contributing rollouts $(m)$ satisfy $\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+1\mid k} \in \mathcal{C}_1^{\mathrm{poly}}$, then the averaged state $\mathbf{x}^\star_{k+1|k}$ under the MPPI-DCBF control is also guaranteed to be in the safe set $\mathcal{C}_1^{\mathrm{poly}}$.
    
    % \begin{proof}
    % Under the control affine dynamics, the state $\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+1\mid k}$ in the next time step is an affine function of the control inputs. Since the admissible inputs are a convex polytope and the control update of MPPI is a convex combination of control perturbations~\eqref{eq:mppi-update}, the resulting state $\mathbf{x}^\star_{k+1\mid k}$ is a convex combination of the rollout states:
    % \begin{align}
    % \mathbf{x}^\star_{k+1|k} = \sum_m \hat{\omega}_m \mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+1|k}, \nonumber
    % \end{align}
    % where $\hat{\omega}_m \in \mathbb{R}_{> 0}$ and $\sum_m \hat{\omega}_m = 1$ are the importance weights derived from \eqref{eq:mppi-weights} after the rejection of unsafe samples. Moreover, since the safe set $\mathcal{C}_1^{\mathrm{poly}}$ is a convex set, any convex combination of points within it must also lie within the set. Thus, if $\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+1\mid k} \in \mathcal{C}_1^{\mathrm{poly}}$ for all $m$, it follows that $\mathbf{x}^\star_{k+1|k} \in \mathcal{C}_1^{\mathrm{poly}}$.
    % \end{proof}
\end{proposition}

If the dynamics of the system of interest are linear time-invariant (LTI) as

\setlength{\abovedisplayshortskip}{2pt}

\begin{equation}

\end{equation}
then we can make a similar statement about safety along the \textit{entire} trajectory, rather than just a single step in the MPPI averaging process.

\begin{proposition} \label{prop:2}
For the discrete LTI system \eqref{eq:lti} under the same pointwise feasibility assumption, if all contributing rollouts $(m)$ satisfy $\mathbf{x}^{(m)}_{k+i\mid k} \in \mathcal{C}_i^{\mathrm{poly}}$ for all steps $i \in \{1, \dots, H\}$, then the entire averaged MPPI trajectory also remains within the sequence of safe sets, \textit{i.e.,} $\mathbf{x}^\star_{k+i\mid k} \in \mathcal{C}_i^{\mathrm{poly}}$ for all $i$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Motion Planning in General Obstacle Worlds}
\label{sec:futurework}

Our framework so far assumes circular obstacles and a single body point, as is common in safety-critical planning~\cite{agrawal2017discrete, mizuta2025unified}. We here show that our MPPI-DCBF can be extended to more general motion planning problems. For a convex obstacle, the affine barrier function~\eqref{eq:aff_barrier} can be easily defined to its closest boundary point~\cite{zhang2020optimization,deits2015computing}, thus the nominal polytope can be well defined. Moreover, a non-convex obstacle can be modeled as a union of convex components, a common modeling convention in physics-based simulation environments~\cite{todorov2012mujoco,liu2023libero}, as $\mathcal O_j=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{L_j}\mathcal O_{j,\ell}$.
The robot may also carry several body points, as required in full-body safety~\cite{wilkinson2026full}. Let $\pi_c$ map the state to a body point $\q^{(c)}=\pi_c(\x)$, with body Jacobian $J_c=\partial \pi_c/\partial \x \in \mathbb R^{3\times n}$. Each of the total $N_c$ points sampled on the robot, linearized at the current state as 
\begin{equation}
\pi_c(\x)\approx\pi_c(\xk)+J_c(\xk)(\x-\xk)
\end{equation}
Each pair of a body point and a component then contributes one affine barrier function, then nominal safety function can be extended as
\begin{equation}
h^{\mathrm{poly}}(\x;\xk)\!=\!\min_{c\le N_c}\min_{j\in\mathcal O(\xk)}\min_{\ell\le L_j}
h^{\mathrm{aff}}_{j,\ell}\big(\pi_c(\x);\xk\big).
\label{eq:poly_multi}
\end{equation}
Taking a minimum of these affine functions keeps every $\mathcal C_i^{\mathrm{poly}}$ convex, so Assumption~\ref{assumption_1} and Propositions~\ref{prop:1} and~\ref{prop:2} can be extended to this general case for the linearized body points. Our setup for circular obstacles with a single body point is recovered with $N_c=1$ and $\pi_c=\pi_q$.

As a preliminary study, we run MPPI-DCBF with extended nominal polytope~\eqref{eq:poly_multi} on a Franka FR3 arm in \emph{LIBERO}~\cite{liu2023libero}, a manipulation benchmark whose objects are composed of unions of convex box primitives. We use $N_c=183$ body points on the end effector, distal links, and fingers, a joint-velocity integrator with $\Delta t=0.08$\,s, with Jacobians computed once per replan. Proposition~\ref{prop:2} certifies the averaged plan for the linearized body points.
%only, and an exact nonlinear recheck shrinks the horizon toward zero control when it fails. 
We omit a 3-D visualization of~\eqref{eq:poly_multi} because, for a general articulated robot, the safe set is defined by constraints on multiple body points in the robot’s high-dimensional configuration space and cannot be faithfully represented in 3-D position space.
Fig.~\ref{fig:robotarm} shows collision-free trajectories for all tested $\gamma$ with $H=10$ and $M=1024$ rollouts. The minimum clearance is smallest at $\gamma=1.0$ and largest at $\gamma=0.1$.

\subsection{Multi-step Safety Verification}
We define a deterministic verifier that tests whether a queried action admits a finite-horizon DCBF certificate (\textit{i.e.,} multi-step safety).

\noindent \textbf{Verifier Polytope for Expansion.}
The nominal polytope~\eqref{eq:poly_safeset} is shared by all nonzero-weight rollouts, which makes the averaging argument work. In a nonconvex free space, however, a single shared region may exclude collision-free rollouts in other local modes, even when enlarged~\cite{deits2015computing}. Therefore, to expand a generative policy into these unexplored safe modes, we design the verifier to keep the same conditions but build the separating faces around the queried action, thereby verifying the actions the original planner excludes (See Fig.~\ref{fig:pedestrian}(a)).

For a queried action \(\hat{\mathbf u}\), let
\(\{\xki\}_{i=0}^{H}\) denotes the resulting trajectory. For each convex configuration-space obstacle \(j\in\mathcal O(\xk)\), represented as \(\mathcal O_j:=\{\q\in\mathbb R^d:\|\q-\oj\|_2\le r_j\},\)
the verifier constructs a separating hyperplane \(\mathbf a_j^\top(\mathbf q-\qk)\le m_j,\)
by solving the maximization problem
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\max_{\aj, m_j}\ & m_j\\
\text{s.t. }\ & \aj^\top(\qki-\qk)\le\big(1-(1-\gamma)^i\big)m_j \; (i=1,\dots,H),\\
& r_j\|\aj\|_2\le \aj^\top(\oj-\qk)-m_j,\\
&\|\aj\|_2\le1,\ 0< m_{\min} \le m_j.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:socp}
\end{equation}
Problem~\eqref{eq:socp} is a second-order cone program (SOCP), solved with Clarabel through CVXPY~\cite{goulart2026clarabel}. Specifically, the first trajectory constraint imposes the safety specification in \eqref{eq:poly} with \(h(\xki;\xk)=1-\aj^\top(\qki-\qk)/m_j\) equal to 1 at the current position and is required to remain at least $(1-\gamma)^i$. The separation constraint follows from
\[
\inf_{\q\in\mathcal O_j}\aj^\top(\q-\qk)
=
\aj^\top(\oj-\qk)-r_j\|\aj\|_2
\ge m_j.
\]
The remaining constraints fix the scale of the normal and impose the strictly positive margin $m_{\min}\!>\!0$. With $(\aj^\star,m_j^\star)$ a solution for obstacle $j$. The resulting verifier polytope \footnote{For brevity, we abbreviate the \(H\) steps of predicted control sequence (or action) as $\{ \hat{\u}_{k+i\mid k}\}_{i=0}^{H-1}$ as \(\hat{\mathbf u}\). Also, we augment both the nominal and verifier polytope constructions with auxiliary half-spaces that bound the sensing region so that $\mathcal P$ is bounded (12 faces in Fig.~\ref{fig:pedestrian}(a)).} for a queried action is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P(\hat{\mathbf u};\o,\gamma):=\nspace{-10}\bigcap_{j\in\mathcal O(\xk)}\nspace{-10}\{
\q\in\mathbb R^d:
{\aj^\star}^{\!\top}(\q-\qk)\le m_j^\star\}.
\label{eq:verifierpolytope}
\end{equation}

Having formulated the optimization problem to verify the existence of a multi-step safety specification, we formally define the \textit{validity} of a queried action.
\begin{definition}
\label{def:verifier}
Let \(v_\gamma(\hat{\mathbf u};\o) \in \{0, 1\}\) be a indicator function that equals $1$ if and only if~\eqref{eq:socp} is feasible for \emph{every} $j\in\mathcal O(\xk)$ at action $\hat{\mathbf u}$, context $\o$, and safety parameter $\gamma$.
The \textit{valid action set} is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)
    :=
    \left\{
    \hat{\mathbf u}\in\mathcal U^H:
    v_\gamma(\hat{\mathbf u};\o)=1
    \right\},
    \label{eq:verifier-certified-set}
\end{equation}
and action \(\hat{\mathbf u} \in \Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)\) is a \textit{valid} action that guarantees the existence of a verifier polytope~\eqref{eq:verifierpolytope}.
\end{definition}

Thus, a predicted trajectory of a valid action is collision-free, and the feasibility of~\eqref{eq:socp} is decided at each planning step. Under the condition below, every action satisfying the nominal polytope constraints~\ref{eq:nominalpolytope} is a valid action.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:nominal-verifier}
Fix $(\o,\gamma)$ and suppose the configuration-space
obstacles are represented as above. Let $\p_j$ and the
outward unit normal $\n_j$ define the nominal face, and let \(d_j:=\n_j^\top(\qk-\p_j)=\|\qk-\oj\|_2-r_j>0.\)
If $m_{\min}\le\min_{j\in\mathcal O(\xk)}d_j$, then, for every
$\hat{\mathbf u}\in\mathcal U^H$ whose resulting trajectory satisfies
\[
h^{\mathrm{poly}}(\xki;\xk)\ge(1-\gamma)^i,
\quad i=1,\ldots,H,
\]
the choice $(\aj,m_j)=(-\n_j,d_j)$ is feasible
for~\eqref{eq:socp}, and hence
$\hat{\mathbf u}\in\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$.
\end{proposition}
This implication holds for MPPI-DCBF under Assumption~\ref{assumption_1} and the LTI conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:2}. For a general control-affine system, each queried action must be verified separately. 
In the following proposition, we show the effect of $\gamma$ during the verification process.
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:gamma-effect}
For any $\o$ and
$0<\gamma_1\le\gamma_2\le1$,
\setlength{\abovedisplayskip}{2pt}
\setlength{\abovedisplayshortskip}{2pt}
\begin{equation}
\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma_1)
\subseteq
\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma_2).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
For every horizon $i$,
$1-(1-\gamma)^i$ is nondecreasing in $\gamma$.
Thus, any valid action at $\gamma_1$ also satisfies the trajectory constraints at $\gamma_2$, no other constraint of~\eqref{eq:socp} involves $\gamma$. \hfill$\blacksquare$

While Proposition~\ref{prop:nominal-verifier} formally guarantees that the MPPI-DCBF planner gives valid action priors, it inherently yields a subset of the distribution of all possible safe actions at a given context and required safety level (\textit{i.e.,} $\gamma$). This fundamental limitation strongly motivates us to expand the generative policy distribution beyond the initial demonstrations to discover a broader range of valid actions.

\subsection{Safe Flow Expansion}
We introduce Safe Flow Expansion (Alg.~\ref{alg:sfe}) for the CFM model, which acquires missing safe behavioral modes by first pretraining on the certified actions of MPPI-DCBF and iteratively increasing the probability mass on uncertain multi-step safe actions. 

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Safe Flow Expansion}
\label{alg:sfe}
\small
\LinesNumbered
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{pretrained CFM $\theta_0$; verifier $v_{\gamma_k}$; dynamics $f$;
execution cost $C$; rounds $N$; success target $P$;
proposal and verification budgets $K\ge B$}
\KwOut{expanded CFM $\theta_N$}
$\mathcal B_0\gets\varnothing$\;
\For{$n\gets0$ \KwTo $N-1$}{
$\sigma_n\gets\textsc{UpdateUncertainty}(\mathcal B_n)$;\quad
$\mathcal D_n,\mathcal S_n\gets\varnothing$\;
\Repeat{$|\mathcal S_n|\ge P$}{
roll out $\theta_n$ in parallel\;
\ForEach{replanning step $k$ with context $(\o_k,\gamma_k)$}{
draw $\hat{\mathbf u}^{(1:B)}$ from $K$ proposals
by~\eqref{eq:importancesampling}\;
$y_j\gets v_{\gamma_k}(\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j)};\o_k)$,
$\;\mathcal J_k^+\gets\{j:y_j=1\}$\;
$\mathcal D_n\gets\mathcal D_n\cup
\{(\o_k,\gamma_k,\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j)},y_j)\}_{j=1}^{B}$\;
\lIf{$\mathcal J_k^+=\varnothing$}{\textsc{RestartRollout}()}
$j^\star\gets\arg\min_{j\in\mathcal J_k^+}
C(\o_k,\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j)})$\;
$\mathbf u_k\gets[\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j^\star)}]_0$,
$\;\mathbf x_{k+1}\gets f(\mathbf x_k,\mathbf u_k)$\;
}
add every restart-free rollout to $\mathcal S_n$\;
}
$\mathcal B_{n+1}\gets\textsc{UpdateBuffer}(\mathcal B_n,\mathcal D_n)$;\quad

$\theta_{n+1}\gets\textsc{UpdateCFM}(\theta_n,\mathcal D_n^{+})$\;
}
\Return{$\theta_N$}\;
% \vskip -0.1 true in
\end{algorithm}

Concretely, for a fixed \((\o,\gamma)\), the policy at expansion round \(n\) induces the \textit{conditional generable set} associated with the probability density lower bound \(\tau\) defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_{\theta_n}^{\tau}(\o,\gamma)
    :=\{
    \hat{\mathbf u}\in\mathcal U^H:
    p_1^{\theta_n}(\hat{\mathbf u}\mid\o,\gamma)\ge\tau
    \},
    \label{eq:conditional-generable-set}
\end{equation}
where $p_1^{\theta_n}(\mathbf u\mid\o,\gamma)$ denotes the conditional policy density at round $n$. The conditional generable set contains actions to which the policy assigns density at least $\tau$. Here, we recover the pre-trained conditional generable set by setting \(n=0\).
\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{images/demo_3d/beta.pdf}
    \vspace{-6.0mm}
    \caption{Rollouts of the expanded policy in the 3D single sphere task after SFE, run with the same parameters but for $\beta=0.1$, $\beta=1.0$, and $\beta\to\infty$ (standard sampling at the current policy, $\hat{\mathbf u} \sim p_1^{\theta_n}$). Colors show the four modes: above, below, left, and right. Smaller $\beta$ puts more weight on uncertain actions and discovers new trajectories.}
    \label{fig:beta}
    \vspace{-\baselineskip}
\end{figure}
To self-generate samples informative about multi-step safety while staying near the current policy \(\theta_n\), at expansion round \(n\),
we sample\footnote{The exact Gibbs solution of
\(
p_n \in \arg\max_q \{ \mathbb{E}_q[\sigma_n(\phi_s(\hat\u))] - \beta \text{KL}(q \parallel p_1^{\theta_n})\},
\) with $\text{KL}$ denotes Kullback--Leibler divergence.}
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf u}
\sim
p_1^{\theta_n}(\hat{\mathbf u}\mid\o,\gamma)
\exp\!\left(
\sigma_n\!\left(\phi_s(\hat{\mathbf u};\o,\gamma)\right)/\beta
\right)
/Z_n(\o,\gamma)
,
\label{eq:importancesampling}
\end{equation}
where $\beta$ is the parameter that controls the trade-off between exploration and the prior, $Z_n(\o,\gamma)$ is the normalizing constant \footnote{Define \(
Z_n(\o,\gamma)\!=\!\int_{\mathcal U^H}
p_1^{\theta_n}(\mathbf u\!\mid\!\o,\!\gamma)\exp\!\left(
\sigma_n\!\left(\phi_s(\mathbf u;\o,\!\gamma)\right)/\beta
\right)
d\mathbf u.
\)}, $\phi_s$ denotes the penultimate layer feature of the conditional flow vector field evaluated at the fixed flow-noise level $s$, and $\sigma_n$ is the kernel posterior uncertainty returned by \textsc{UpdateUncertainty} in Alg.~\ref{alg:sfe}. We modeled $\sigma_n$ as the posterior standard deviation of a Gaussian process fitted on the features $\phi_s$ to the binary observations in the buffer. For $\phi=\phi_s(\hat{\mathbf u};\o,\gamma)$, define $[\mathbf K_n]_{ij}=k(\phi_i,\phi_j)$ and $[\mathbf k_n(\phi)]_i=k(\phi_i,\phi)$. The posterior variance is
\begin{equation}
\sigma_n^2(\phi)=k(\phi,\phi)-\mathbf k_n(\phi)^{\!\top}\left(\mathbf K_n+\nu I\right)^{-1}\mathbf k_n(\phi),
\label{eq:gp-uncertainty}
\end{equation}
where $\nu\!>\!0$ is a regularization parameter. We use its posterior standard deviation as a feature-space uncertainty score.

At each replanning step $k$, SFE self-generates a batch of actions from the current CFM policy using the GP posterior uncertainty $\sigma_n$ in ~\eqref{eq:importancesampling} within the parallel rollouts. The verifier $v_{\gamma_k}$, defined by the finite-horizon DCBF conditions in Def.~\ref{def:verifier}, separates these queried actions $\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j)}$ into valid (\textit{i.e.,} $j\in\mathcal J_k^+$) and invalid sets, and adds both sets to $\mathcal D_n$. If no valid action exists, the rollout restarts. The execution cost \(C\) ranks valid action candidates, from which the first action with minimum cost is executed~\footnote{One can set this cost as expert actions \cite{ross2011reduction} or restrict execution to a safe admissible action set \cite{alshiekh2018safe}.}. Even when $C$ is shared across, queried actions depend on $\gamma$ through the admissible set $\mathcal J_k^+$, thereby preserving the semantics of $\gamma$ (\textit{i.e.,} safety--performance tradeoff) through policy expansion. 

We repeat this data acquisition until we gather $P$ successful trajectories. Both valid and invalid sets are then added to the buffer through \textsc{UpdateBuffer}, from which \textsc{UpdateUncertainty} constructs $\sigma_{n+1}$ in the next round. Finally, \textsc{UpdateCFM} uses hierarchical sampling~\cite{idrissi2022simple} among valid queried actions $\mathcal D_n^+ \subset \mathcal D_n$, uniformly over $\gamma$ and successful trajectories, and regresses the CFM loss~\eqref{eq:cfm-loss} on these uncertainty-guided data.
 
Because the buffer \(\mathcal B_n\) pools observations from finite contexts at each round, \(\sigma_n\) may prioritize an action due to novelty in unrelated contexts. However, no finite buffer can cover the space of planning contexts. The same issue makes generative planners struggle to generalize to OOD environments without retraining \cite{ye2024efficient, dai2025safefm}. We therefore use the buffer only as an acquisition heuristic to actively explore unvisited modes \cite{de2026active}. Thus, it can affect the query but not validity.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol and Implementation}
\noindent\textbf{Evaluation protocol.}

MPPI-DCBF generates all pretraining data under 2D and 3D double-integrator dynamics and a sampling time of $\Delta t = 0.1s$. Each of the four tasks runs three phases: in-distribution training, SFE (Alg.~\ref{alg:sfe}) in OOD environments, and final OOD testing, with start, goal, and dynamics fixed~\footnote{In \emph{SFE adaptation}, expansion and evaluation use the same OOD obstacle configuration (2D cluttered environment and 3D single-sphere environment). To account for the varying difficulty of the four tasks, we use a task-specific number $P$ of successful trajectories, fixed across rounds. After each round $n$, we independently evaluate the CFM model $\theta_n$ with a 16-step ODE solver on 100 test rollouts per task and $\gamma$.

\noindent \textbf{Task configurations.} \emph{2D cluttered environment (Fig.~\ref{fig:2d_cluttered})}: start \((0.3,0.3)\), goal \((4.7,4.7)\), \(5\,\mathrm{m}\times5\,\mathrm{m}\) taskspace, pretrained on 200k MPPI-DCBF actions of length $H$ from random initial positions to the fixed goal among regularly sized circular obstacles. Testing replaces the central obstacles with one unseen large obstacle that blocks the short demonstrated paths and requires long-horizon planning out of local minima. 
% In the \emph{2D cluttered environment}, the robot starts at \((0.3,0.3)\) and navigates toward the fixed goal at \((4.7,4.7)\) in a \(5\,\mathrm{m}\times5\,\mathrm{m}\) taskspace. The CFM model is pretrained on an MPPI-DCBF policy from a random initial position to a fixed goal position, total 200k actions of length $H$ as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:2d_cluttered}, in a nominal environment with regularly sized circular obstacles. We tested this by replacing the central obstacles with a previously unseen large obstacle to see whether SFE can learn long-horizon, collision-free behavior and avoid getting trapped in local minima.

\noindent \textbf{Implementation details:} 
The CFM velocity network is one MLP with 3 hidden layers. It is conditioned on $\gamma$ and the context: the relative goal position, the velocity, and local observation at the current time step $h^\text{poly}$ (or its 10-step history in the dynamic-pedestrian task) within a \(2\mathrm{m}\) sensing range. A convolutional neural network (CNN) downsamples the angular axes of this discretized observation grid while preserving its radial resolution, and compresses it into a 64-dim observation token. We fix $O=1$, $H=10$, and $A=1$.

At each SFE step, the CFM policy self-generates $K=32$ proposals and resamples $B=16$ of them for verification under~\eqref{eq:importancesampling}, doubled to \((64,32)\) in the dynamic-pedestrian task. Here $K$ controls diversity and $B$ bounds the verification cost. We set the execution cost $C$ to the standard MPC cost~\eqref{eq:mppicost} which selects the executed action among the verified candidates. Uncertainty is the posterior of an RBF Gaussian process (GP) on the $L_2$-normalized 32-dim $\phi_s$ at flow time $s=0.9$ with a task-specific $\beta$ and length scale calibrated from pretrained embeddings. 

Additionally, in the dynamic-pedestrian task, the verifier certifies validity with respect to the currently observed obstacle geometry, frozen over the prediction horizon. We use a constant-velocity model of the pedestrians only in the execution cost to favor valid action candidates that anticipate future collisions. Consequently, avoidance of moving pedestrians beyond the frozen-scene certificate is empirical and relies on receding-horizon replanning. 

At deployment, SFE generates \(J\) parallel actions and ranks them with the same cost, with \(J=1\) by default for validity and mode discovery, and \(J>1\) in~\Cref{sec:tradeoff} to study its effect on safety and performance. 
%, employing Adam with learning rate $1 \times 10^{-4}$, batch 64, and 4 inner steps. 


\noindent \textbf{Baseline method} We compare against mode-selective CFM-MPPI~\cite{mizuta2025unified}, which combines reward-guided flow generation with local MPPI refinement.  Given a base pre-trained vector field $\mathbf{v}^{\theta_{\text{0}}}(\mathbf{x}\!\mid\! \mathbf{o},\gamma)$, the guided velocity field is the local gradient ascent of the combined reward:
\begin{equation}
   \tilde{\mathbf{v}}^{\theta_{\text{0}}}(\mathbf{x}_\tau\!\mid\! \mathbf{o},\gamma)  = \mathbf{v}^{\theta_{\text{0}}}(\mathbf{x}_\tau\!\mid\! \mathbf{o},\gamma) \;+\; \eta \nabla_{\mathbf{z}_1}R(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z}_1),
   \label{eq:cfm-mppi_vectorfield}
\end{equation}
where the reward $R$ encourages both performance and safety,
\begin{equation}
    R(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\hat u}) = \sum_{k=0}^{H-1} w_\text{s} r_\text{s}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\hat u}) + w_\text{g} r_\text{g} (\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\hat u}),
    \label{eq:cfm-mppi_guidance}
\end{equation}
and $w_\text{s}, w_\text{g}$ are scalar weights. The safety reward term $r_\text{s}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\hat u})$ is defined to penalize violations of the CBF condition~\cite[Eq. 6]{mizuta2025unified}, whereas the performance reward $r_\mathrm{g}$ is a goal reaching term. CFM-MPPI generates $J=32$ guided priors at each step, refines the eight lowest-cost priors using $M=32$ Gaussian perturbations each, and executes the first control of the lowest-cost refined sequence. It warm-starts the next replan at flow time $t=0.75$. For each task, we selected the weight pair with the best trade-off between safety (collision rate) and task performance (time-to-goal).

\subsection{Safe Flow Expansion Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

This section connects the MPPI-DCBF demonstrations to the initial set for SFE and a conditional lower bound on valid-action availability (Details on the fixed-context analysis model and generable set coverage are deferred to~\Cref{app:sfe-analysis}). All results apply separately to each fixed $(\o,\gamma)$, and extending them to closed-loop operation over sequences of changing OOD contexts remains future work.

For a validity threshold $p_{\min}\in(0,1)$ and $\epsilon\in(0,1-p_{\min})$, we denote a measurable $S_0\subseteq\mathcal U^H$ an \textit{initial safe set} if $\operatorname{Vol}(S_0)$ is well defined and positive, $S_0\subseteq\Omega^{\tau}_{\theta_0}(\o,\gamma)\cap\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$, and $\inf_{\hat{\mathbf u}\in S_0}\varsigma(g(\phi_s(\hat{\mathbf u};\o,\gamma))) \ge p_{\min}+\epsilon$. Let $R_\epsilon$ denote the induced action-space one-step reachability operator of~\cite[Eq.~(19)]{de2026active} \footnote{In our notation, this operator maps a set $S\subseteq\mathcal U^H$ of actions to \(R_\epsilon(S):=\{\hat{\mathbf u}\in\mathcal U^H:\ \exists\,\hat{\mathbf u}'\in S\ \text{s.t.}\varsigma(g(\phi_s(\hat{\mathbf u}';\o,\gamma)))-L_\varsigma L_g\,d(\hat{\mathbf u},\hat{\mathbf u}')-\epsilon\ \ge\ p_{\min}\},\) where $d(\hat{\mathbf u},\hat{\mathbf u}'):=\|\phi_s(\hat{\mathbf u};\o,\gamma)-\phi_s(\hat{\mathbf u}';\o,\gamma)\|_2$ is the feature distance, and $L_\varsigma$ and $L_g$ are the Lipschitz constants of $\varsigma$ and $g$ in the analysis model.} with $\mathcal X=\mathcal U^H$ and $\phi=\phi_s(\cdot;\o,\gamma)$. We use  $R_\epsilon^r$ for $r$ repeated applications of this operator.

The MPPI-DCBF demonstrations provide only finitely many verified actions, which by themselves have zero volume in the action space and therefore cannot form the initial set required by the expansion analysis. The following lemma bridges this gap, where strict feasibility and continuity enlarge each verified action into a small safe neighborhood, whose union provides a nonzero-volume initial safe set for SFE.

\begin{lemma}[Initial safe set from MPPI-DCBF]
\label{lem:seed}
Under the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:nominal-verifier}, let $Q\ge1$ and let $\{\hat{\mathbf u}_0^{(q)}\}_{q=1}^{Q}\subset\operatorname{int}(\mathcal U^H)$ be a finite set of MPPI-DCBF actions satisfying~\eqref{eq:nominalpolytope} strictly, with $p_1^{\theta_0}(\hat{\mathbf u}_0^{(q)}\mid\o,\gamma)>\tau$ and $\varsigma(g(\phi_s(\hat{\mathbf u}_0^{(q)};\o,\gamma)))>p_{\min}+\epsilon$. 
If the dynamics in~\eqref{eq:affinesystem} are continuous in the state and action,
% If the resulting finite-horizon trajectory depends continuously on $\hat{\mathbf u}$ at these actions, 
and
$p_1^{\theta_0}(\cdot\mid\o,\gamma)$ and $\varsigma\circ g\circ\phi_s$ are
continuous, then there exist radii $\rho_q>0$ such that
$S_0:=\bigcup_q B_{\rho_q}(\hat{\mathbf u}_0^{(q)})$ is an initial safe set \footnote{Here, \(B_\rho(\bar{\mathbf u})\) denotes the open Euclidean ball \(\left\{\hat{\mathbf u}\!\in\!\mathcal U^H\!:\!\|\hat{\mathbf u}\!-\!\bar{\mathbf u}\|_2\!<\!\rho\right\}\).}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By continuity of the dynamics in~\eqref{eq:affinesystem}, the induced finite-horizon trajectory map is continuous in $\hat{\mathbf u}$. 
Since all relevant conditions are strict, they remain true for actions sufficiently close to each $\hat{\mathbf u}_0^{(q)}$. Choose each $\rho_q$ small enough that its ball remains inside $\mathcal U^H$ and preserves these conditions. \Cref{prop:nominal-verifier} then places every such ball in $\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:seed} requires a finite set of actions that satisfies the strict conditions and remains valid under sufficiently small action changes. Starting from this initial safe set $S_0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:coverage} guarantees that after $T^\star=rT$ sequential query of model updates at a fixed $(\o,\gamma)$ after which the coverage event $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{cov}}\!:=\!\{R_\epsilon^r(S_0)\!\subseteq\!\Omega_{\theta_{T^\star}}^\tau\!(\o,\gamma)\}$ holds with probability at least $1-\delta$, \textit{i.e.,} $\Pr(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{cov}})\ge1-\delta$. This gives the following sampling guarantee.

\begin{corollary}[Probability of valid action after expansion]
\label{thm:safe-action-availability}
Under the setting above, let $r\!\ge\!1$ be an integer and define
$\mathcal A_{\gamma,r}(\o)\!:=\!R_\epsilon^r(S_0)\cap\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$
and $m_{\gamma,r}(\o):=\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal A_{\gamma,r}(\o))$. Then $m_{\gamma,r}(\o)\ge\operatorname{Vol}(S_0)>0$, and $m_{\gamma,r}(\o)$ is nondecreasing in $r$. 
Conditioned on the expansion history, independently sample $J\ge1$ actions from $p_1^{\theta_{T^\star}}(\cdot\mid\o,\gamma)$. Accounting for both the expansion process and the $J$ samples, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\Pr\!\left[\exists\,\!j\!\le\! J\!:\! v_\gamma(\hat{\mathbf u}^{(j)};\!\o)\!=\!1\right]
% &\!\ge\!
% (1-\delta)\!\left[1-\!\bigl(1\!-\!\tau m_{\gamma,r}(\o)\!\bigr)^{J}\right]\!\\
\!\ge\!
(1-\delta)\!\left[1\!-\!e^{\!-J\tau m_{\gamma,r}(\o)}\right]\!\!.
\label{eq:main1}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By definition, the reachability operator contains its input and preserves set inclusion. Therefore, it holds that $S_0\subseteq\!R_\epsilon^r(S_0)\subseteq\! R_\epsilon^{r+1}(S_0)$. Intersecting with $\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)\supseteq S_0$ gives the volume bound. On the coverage event $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{cov}}$, every action in $\mathcal A_{\gamma,r}(\o)$ is a valid action and has density at least $\tau$. Then, by the independence of the $J$ sample, we get the conditional bound \(1-\left(1-\tau m_{\gamma,r}(\o)\right)^J\). Applying \(1\!-\!x\!\le \!e^{-x}\) and $\Pr(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{cov}})\ge1-\delta$ gives~\eqref{eq:main1}.
\end{proof}

The lower bound in~\eqref{eq:main1} is nondecreasing with $r$ as $m_{\gamma,r}(\o)$ is nondecreasing by the monotonicity of the reachability operator, for fixed $(\o,\gamma,S_0,\epsilon,\tau,\delta,J)$~\footnote{This conclusion neither assumes that the conditional generable set for one $r$ is contained in the next nor uses the same coverage condition for different $r$. The claim applies only to the guaranteed lower bound, and it does not claim that the validity of each later CFM policy must increase.}. Also, not only the effect of $r$ but also the effect of $\gamma$ can be noted. Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-effect} establishes an inclusion between valid action sets $\Omega^\star(\o,\gamma)$ at different values of $\gamma$, whereas Corollary~\ref{thm:safe-action-availability} applies separately to each fixed $(\o,\gamma)$~\footnote{Changing $\gamma$ may also change the conditional policy, learned representation, initial safe set, and reachability operator.}. Therefore, Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-effect} by itself does not imply an inclusion between the corresponding reachable or conditional generable sets, and hence does not imply monotonicity of $m_{\gamma,r}(\o)$ or~\eqref{eq:main1} in $\gamma$.

\section{Conclusion}
Safe Flow Expansion raises verifier validity over the pretrained policy in four OOD environments and discovers new modes absent from the demonstrations. With self-generation and verification offline, the expanded policy replans at a fraction of the baseline methods. We also demonstrated that the safety parameter $\gamma$ tunes both the online planner and the expanded policy along a clearance/time-to-goal frontier.

Learning an optimal $\gamma$ schedule directly from data remains an interesting open problem (\textit{e.g.,} rather than a fixed $\gamma$ during deployment). Such dynamic scheduling would allow the system to tightly trade off safety and performance, which can be critical in real-world tasks where the robot must adjust caution to context.

Under the assumptions of~\Cref{sec:analysis}, Corollary~\ref{thm:safe-action-availability} provides lower bounds, with high probability, at a fixed context and $\gamma$, on the probability that a generated batch contains a valid action. Future work could explore combining our expanded policy with a lightweight run-time safety filter to provide hard guarantees. Our next step is to scale our framework to systems where expert demonstrations are prohibitively expensive, such as safety-critical manipulation, and to continue preliminary study of~\Cref{sec:futurework}.
